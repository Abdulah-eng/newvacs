WEEK 5 UAT / QA BUG REPORT
Major Depressive Disorder + Generalized Anxiety Disorder + Tobacco Cessation Module
Developer / IT handoff — clinical data, patient-case mapping, grading, UI, and technical issues
Purpose of this report
This report translates the Week 5 clinical/student audit into developer-facing QA findings. The goal is to identify discrepancies between the information available to the student through the patient interview and EHR and the information expected by the grading/reference system.
The report focuses on issues that may affect clinical accuracy, student experience, grading reliability, case consistency, and usability. Each issue is organized by encounter and patient and identifies the observed behavior, expected behavior, potential impact, and recommended correction.
Screenshot evidence should be placed immediately after the corresponding issue whenever available.
Most important overall finding
The most significant Week 5 issue is a recurring mismatch between the student-visible patient encounter/EHR and the information contained in the grading key. In multiple cases, students are being evaluated against information that the patient did not provide, information that directly contradicts the patient response, or objective data that differ from the values displayed in the EHR.
This creates a grading environment in which a student can accurately document the patient encounter and still lose points because the grading system is using a different version of the patient's history, symptoms, medication use, adherence, vitals, laboratory data, or follow-up status.
A second major concern is that several rubric items require students to document information that is not obtainable from the patient-facing encounter, such as unavailable laboratory values, unreported social history, undisclosed psychiatric symptoms, or historical diagnoses/procedures that the patient specifically denies or does not report.
Executive Summary
Patient-facing EHR information does not consistently match the grading key.
Multiple tobacco-use histories conflict between the EHR and standardized-patient responses.
Vitals used by the grading system do not consistently match the vitals displayed in the student-facing Vitals tab.
Students are deducted for laboratory documentation even when no laboratory values are available in the chart.
Students are being graded on information the patient did not provide, including social history, prior therapy, medical conditions, psychiatric symptoms, medication discontinuation, and medication adherence.
Several grading criteria appear to expect the patient to provide a specific answer rather than grading the student's documentation of the actual patient response.
Multiple patients have medication-history discrepancies between the patient interview, medication list, previous encounters, and grading key.
Follow-up interval information is inconsistent between the patient chart and grading key.
Patient demographic information is internally inconsistent.
Some grading criteria appear to deduct points for details that were not clinically required or were not stated by the patient.
These issues create a risk that students will be penalized for accurately documenting the information available during the encounter.

W5-01 — Knowledge-quiz answer choices are difficult to read [LOW] Current / observed
The answer choices for the Monday knowledge quiz are displayed closely together without sufficient spacing between options I–IV. This makes the question visually difficult to read and can make it harder for students to distinguish where one answer choice ends and the next begins.
Expected / grading reference
Answer choices should have sufficient vertical spacing and visual separation so that each option can be easily identified.

This is primarily a usability issue rather than a clinical or grading error. Poor formatting can increase cognitive load and may make students more likely to misread or accidentally select an answer.

Add consistent vertical spacing between answer choices I–IV. Consider using standard list formatting, increased line height, and/or indentation for each response option. Verify readability on both desktop and smaller screens.

Cross-Cutting Grading Logic Concerns
Key distinction for the developer
The Week 5 audit demonstrates a recurring distinction between accurate student documentation and matching a predetermined hidden reference answer.
These should not be treated as equivalent.
Patient responses should be the source of truth
When the standardized patient gives a specific response, the student should be evaluated on whether the SOAP note accurately reflects that response.
Examples from this audit include:
Sarah reporting no specific smoking triggers while the key expects work-related stress triggers.
Jessica reporting 3–4 missed doses per week while the key expects 2–3.
Jessica reporting 100% sertraline adherence for 3 weeks while the key expects continued missed doses.
David reporting little improvement while the key expects significant improvement.
David denying previous CBT while the key expects previous CBT.
Sarah denying previous quit attempts while the key expects a prior attempt.
Information unavailable to the student should not be graded as an omission
If the patient does not provide a value and the EHR does not contain it, the student should be able to document:
“Unknown”
“Not provided”
“Not assessed”
“Not documented”
“Patient unable to recall”
without losing points solely because the hidden reference contains a specific answer.
Objective data must have one source of truth
The repeated vital-sign discrepancies strongly suggest that objective data may exist in multiple locations or versions.
The system should have one authoritative encounter object for:
Vitals
Laboratory values
Weight
Medication list
Medication adherence
Diagnoses
Tobacco status
Follow-up interval
Relevant medical history
The EHR and grading engine should both reference the same data.
Do not grade students on unavailable laboratory values
The same laboratory issue occurs repeatedly across Sarah, Jessica, and David.
If no laboratory values are displayed in the student-facing chart, the rubric should not require the student to document them.
Clinical assessment should not be based on hidden patient information
Information such as OSA/CPAP use, previous CBT, cholecystectomy, medication use, migraine history, or other clinical history should be available to the student if it is required for the assessment.
Distinguish documentation from clinical reasoning
The rubric should differentiate between:
Documentation requirement:
“Document the patient's stated smoking triggers.” and
Clinical reasoning requirement:
“Recommend appropriate strategies for managing smoking triggers.”
The first requires a patient response. The second can assess the student's clinical reasoning.
Do not require exact wording
If a student captures the correct clinical meaning, equivalent wording should receive credit. For example, the rubric should generally recognize equivalent documentation of:
No known smoking triggers
Patient denies specific smoking triggers
No identifiable triggers reported
Patient does not associate smoking with specific triggers provided that the underlying clinical information is equivalent.
Recommended Developer / QA Fix Plan
Create one versioned source-of-truth encounter object for every patient and visit.
Ensure the EHR, standardized-patient script, grading key, and SOAP grading engine reference the same patient/version ID.
Synchronize tobacco status across the problem list, social history, patient interview, and grading reference.
Synchronize all medication histories across the medication list, patient interview, previous encounter history, and grading key.
Use the student-visible Vitals tab as the authoritative source for vital-sign grading.
Remove laboratory requirements when no corresponding laboratory data are available to the student.

Final QA Assessment
The Week 5 audit demonstrates a broad source-of-truth and grading-alignment problem
rather than isolated student-documentation errors.
The recurring pattern is that the student-facing encounter and the grading key are sometimes functioning as though they represent different versions of the same patient. In several instances, the student accurately documents information directly obtained from the patient or displayed in the EHR, but the grading system expects different information.
This is particularly important for clinical education because students are being trained to make decisions based on the information available during an encounter. If the grading system rewards a hidden answer rather than the information actually presented to the student, the platform may unintentionally teach students to prioritize the expected answer over accurate patient assessment.
The highest-priority corrective action should therefore be to establish a single,
version-controlled source of truth connecting the patient script, EHR, grading key, rubric, and objective clinical data.
The grading engine should then evaluate the student's documentation against the patient information actually available during that encounter, rather than against information that is hidden, contradictory, or unavailable.
Final QA Note
This report intentionally does not assume that the hidden grading reference is correct simply because it contains a specific value. The primary QA question is whether the student had a reasonable opportunity to obtain the information being graded.
Where the patient-facing EHR and standardized-patient interview provide one set of information and the grading key provides another, the discrepancy should be resolved before the case is released. Where information is unavailable, students should not be penalized for documenting that it was unknown, not provided, or not assessed.
The recommended end state is a synchronized case in which the patient script, EHR, grading rubric, answer key, objective data, and encounter metadata all represent the same patient at the same point in time.
