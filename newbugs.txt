Comprehensive Issue Report and Recommended Fixes Based on the student-facing case, simulated patient interview, AI grading feedback, and Objective-tab screenshot Patient Angela Rodriguez – Thursday 6-month follow-up 1. Purpose and Scope Purpose.  This report documents inconsistencies uncovered while testing an AI-based SOAP note grader. The central concern is that the grader sometimes appears to compare the student's note to hidden/reference facts without first confirming whether those facts were available to the student or whether the simulated patient explicitly contradicted them. Scope.  This report focuses on reproducible grading and case-content issues. The synthetic patient's age discrepancy is intentionally excluded, because the age field is not maintained like a live patient record and was specifically requested to be ignored. 2. Executive Summary Primary systemic defect:  The AI grader does not consistently distinguish between student-accessible evidence and hidden/reference data. As a result, it can penalize clinically accurate, source-grounded documentation and can implicitly reward unsupported or fabricated facts. Direct patient answers are sometimes treated as wrong when they conflict with hidden reference data. Exact historical values are scored even when they were not visible in the chart or obtainable from the patient. The grader does not reliably distinguish unknown/not documented from negative, overdue, or never received. The grader sometimes expects continuation of an activity the patient explicitly says she is not doing. The grader can be too literal about wording/location even when equivalent information is documented elsewhere. Student errors and case-content/reference conflicts are  mixed together  in the same score instead of being separated. The rendered UI and backend/extracted case content may not always match, as demonstrated by the  Immunizations  section. 3. Recommended Core Grading Principle Before deducting points, the grader should determine whether the expected information was  actually available  to the student. For encounter documentation, direct simulated-patient responses and visible student-facing chart data should take precedence over hidden/reference facts. If the hidden reference conflicts with the patient or rendered UI, the grader should flag a case-content discrepancy and apply no student deduction. 4. Recommended AI-Grader Architecture Changes Evidence Availability Gate:  Before scoring a rubric item, classify it as: visible in chart; stated by patient; discoverable if asked; explicitly denied; unknown; patient declined; unavailable; hidden-reference-only; or contradictory. Source-Priority Hierarchy:  For encounter documentation, use this order: direct simulated-patient response → visible current chart → visible historical chart → calculations based on visible inputs → hidden reference. Hidden reference should not override a direct encounter response. Contradiction Detection:  When two sources conflict, stop normal scoring for that criterion. Generate a case-QA alert and avoid a student deduction until the content team resolves the conflict. Unknown-State Logic:  Treat 'unknown', 'not documented', 'patient unsure', and 'blank UI' as distinct from negative findings or overdue status. Conversation-State Awareness:  The grader should know whether the student asked a question, what the patient answered, and whether the answer was positive, negative, unknown, or declined. Semantic Equivalence:  Use meaning-based matching instead of exact wording for living situation, ROS, goals, medication adherence, and patient concerns. No-Fabrication Safeguard:  Do not reward a student for including hidden-reference facts that are unsupported or contradicted by the encounter. UI/Backend Data Parity:  Any fact intended to be graded should be available through the  rendered student experience . Automated tests should compare backend/reference values with what is  actually displayed . Separate Scoring vs QA:  Only student-controllable errors should affect the score. Case/reference/UI conflicts should appear in a separate non-scoring QA section. 7. Suggested Grader Prompt / Policy Text Grade the student's SOAP note using information  actually available  through the student-facing chart and simulated patient encounter. Before deducting points for a missing fact, determine whether the student could reasonably have obtained that fact. Direct responses from the simulated patient and visible student-facing chart data take precedence over hidden/reference data for purposes of encounter documentation. Do not penalize accurate documentation of an explicit denial, unknown history, patient refusal, blank field, or unavailable data. Do not reward unsupported hidden-reference facts that contradict the encounter. When the reference answer conflicts with the patient response or rendered UI, classify the issue as a case-content discrepancy, apply no student deduction, and flag it for review. 8.
