CONFIDENCE Trial: Combination Therapy with Finerenone and Empagliflozin in Chronic Kidney Disease and Type 2 Diabetes
Agarwal R, Filippatos G, Pitt B, et al. Combination Therapy with Finerenone and Empagliflozin in Chronic Kidney Disease and Type 2 Diabetes. N Engl J Med. 2025.
Background
• Chronic kidney disease (CKD) affects millions of patients worldwide and substantially increases the risk of kidney failure, cardiovascular events, hospitalization, and death.
• ACE inhibitors/ARBs and SGLT2 inhibitors have become foundational therapies for diabetic kidney disease; however, substantial residual kidney and cardiovascular risk remains.
• Finerenone has demonstrated renal and cardiovascular benefits in patients with diabetic kidney disease, while SGLT2 inhibitors independently reduce CKD progression and cardiovascular events.
• Prior to CONFIDENCE, limited evidence existed regarding simultaneous initiation of finerenone and SGLT2 inhibitor therapy.
• The CONFIDENCE trial was designed to determine whether combination therapy with finerenone and empagliflozin provides greater reductions in albuminuria than either therapy alone.
Study Objective
To determine whether combination therapy with finerenone and empagliflozin provides superior reduction in albuminuria compared with either therapy alone in patients with chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes.

Author's Conclusion
Among patients with chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes, combination therapy with finerenone and empagliflozin resulted in greater reductions in albuminuria than either therapy alone.
Strengths
• Contemporary CKD population
• Direct comparison of combination versus monotherapy
• Clinically relevant treatment question
• Modern background therapy
• Randomized, double-blind design
• Strong applicability to current practice
Limitations
• Albuminuria surrogate endpoint rather than hard renal outcomes
• Relatively short follow-up period
• Industry funded
• Long-term clinical outcomes remain under investigation
Presenter's Conclusion / Clinical Application
CONFIDENCE is one of the most clinically relevant CKD studies published in recent years because it evaluates a question pharmacists frequently encounter in practice:
"Is residual cardiorenal risk still present after initiating an SGLT2 inhibitor?"
The trial demonstrated that combining finerenone and empagliflozin resulted in substantially greater albuminuria reduction than either therapy alone, supporting the concept of complementary cardiorenal protection.
For pharmacists, CONFIDENCE reinforces the importance of evaluating:
• CKD stage
• Albuminuria
• Residual cardiorenal risk
• Potassium monitoring
• Cardiovascular risk
• Patient-specific barriers to therapy
rather than assuming risk has been adequately addressed with a single intervention.
Patients most likely to benefit include those with:
• Type 2 diabetes
• CKD
• Persistent albuminuria
• Elevated cardiovascular risk
• Residual risk despite ACE inhibitor/ARB therapy

Five Clinical Application Questions
A patient with T2DM, eGFR 45 mL/min/1.73m², UACR 700 mg/g, and potassium 4.5 mEq/L is currently taking losartan and empagliflozin. Would CONFIDENCE support consideration of finerenone?
Answer:
Yes. CONFIDENCE demonstrated greater albuminuria reduction when finerenone was combined with empagliflozin compared with either therapy alone, suggesting additional cardiorenal benefit in high-risk patients.
A patient asks why another medication is being added if they are already taking an SGLT2 inhibitor. How would you explain this?
Answer:
SGLT2 inhibitors reduce kidney and cardiovascular risk but do not eliminate it. Some patients continue to have substantial residual risk, particularly when albuminuria remains elevated. Additional therapies may further reduce that risk.
A patient's potassium increases from 4.6 to 5.0 mEq/L after initiation of finerenone. Does this automatically require discontinuation?
Answer:
No. Potassium should be interpreted in clinical context. Monitoring and assessment of overall benefit versus risk are necessary before making treatment changes.
Why is reduction in albuminuria considered clinically meaningful?
Answer:
Albuminuria is a marker of kidney damage and future CKD progression risk. Reducing albuminuria is associated with improved long-term kidney and cardiovascular outcomes.
