Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF) – Newly Diagnosed, Symptomatic, Not Receiving Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy
The patient has newly diagnosed symptomatic HFrEF as evidenced by an LVEF of 35%, exertional dyspnea, fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, intermittent lower extremity edema, and elevated BNP. Symptoms are most consistent with NYHA Class II heart failure and ACC/AHA Stage C disease.
Current therapy consists of lisinopril and hydrochlorothiazide, neither of which alone represents contemporary guideline-directed medical therapy. According to the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Heart Failure Guideline, patients with symptomatic HFrEF should receive foundational GDMT to reduce mortality, reduce hospitalization, improve symptoms, and slow disease progression.

Assuming ACE inhibitor therapy alone represents adequate heart failure treatment.
Teaching Point
While ACE inhibitors were historically foundational, modern HFrEF management requires implementation of multiple mortality-reducing therapies.
Mistake #2
Focusing exclusively on symptoms.
Teaching Point
Heart failure therapies should reduce:
Mortality
Hospitalizations
Disease progression
not simply improve symptoms.
Mistake #3
Failing to classify heart failure.
Teaching Point
Students should identify:
HFrEF
NYHA Class II
ACC/AHA Stage C
because these classifications drive treatment decisions.
Mistake #4
Neglecting patient education.
Example
"You need to start Entresto and metoprolol."
Teaching Point
Patients need understandable explanations regarding:
Why therapy is changing
Expected benefits
Monitoring requirements
Mistake #5
Ignoring patient fears regarding heart failure.
Teaching Point
Many patients interpret "heart failure" as meaning the heart will stop working immediately.
Addressing misconceptions improves engagement and adherence.
Teaching Pearls
Pearl #1
Patients often normalize early heart failure symptoms.
Expected Discussion
Progressive dyspnea and fatigue are commonly attributed to aging or weight gain before diagnosis.
Pearl #2
Modern HFrEF management focuses on four foundational therapies.
Expected Discussion

ARNI
Evidence-based beta blocker
MRA
SGLT2 inhibitor
as the cornerstone of treatment.
Pearl #3
Rapid implementation is often more important than achieving target doses before adding additional therapies.
Expected Discussion
Current practice favors early initiation of foundational therapies whenever feasible.
Pearl #4
Patient education directly affects outcomes.
Expected Discussion
Medication adherence and self-monitoring frequently determine success more than minor differences in medication selection.
Pearl #5
Heart failure management is longitudinal.
Expected Discussion
The first visit establishes therapy.
Subsequent visits focus on monitoring and optimization.
