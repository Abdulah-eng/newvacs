ASSESSMENT
Asthma – Uncontrolled
The patient has uncontrolled asthma as evidenced by frequent daytime symptoms, albuterol use approximately one to two times daily, nighttime awakenings two to three times weekly, activity limitation, and an ACT score of 16. Pulmonary function testing demonstrates reversible airflow obstruction with a post-bronchodilator FEV1 increase of 14% and 280 mL, supporting the diagnosis of asthma.
According to GINA 2026, patients with asthma should receive inhaled corticosteroid-containing therapy because SABA-only treatment is associated with increased risk of exacerbations and poorer long-term outcomes. The patient's current reliance on albuterol alone is inconsistent with contemporary guideline-directed management.
Given her symptom burden and frequency of reliever use, initiation of low-dose ICS-formoterol maintenance-and-reliever therapy (SMART/MART) is appropriate.
Absence of Controller Therapy
The patient currently relies exclusively on short-acting beta-agonist therapy despite symptoms consistent with persistent asthma.
This represents undertreatment rather than treatment failure. The patient has never received controller therapy and therefore has not received guideline-directed anti-inflammatory treatment.
Initiation of controller therapy is warranted before considering any future treatment escalation.
Allergic Rhinitis – Poorly Controlled
The patient reports persistent allergic rhinitis symptoms including congestion, sneezing, and postnasal drip.
She reports inconsistent use of prescribed intranasal corticosteroid therapy and was unaware that the medication should be used routinely.
Poorly controlled allergic rhinitis may contribute to worsening asthma symptoms and reduced overall respiratory control. Optimization of rhinitis management may improve both upper and lower airway symptoms.
Asthma Knowledge Deficit
The patient demonstrates limited understanding of asthma management and does not understand the distinction between controller and rescue medications.
She also expresses concern regarding inhaled corticosteroid safety and has not previously received formal asthma education.
Patient education is necessary to improve adherence, optimize inhaler use, and support long-term disease control.
