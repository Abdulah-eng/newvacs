ASSESSMENT
1. Chronic Kidney Disease Stage 3a with Severely Increased Albuminuria – High Priority Problem
The patient has chronic kidney disease with an eGFR of 52 mL/min/1.73m², consistent with CKD Stage G3a, and a UACR of 452 mg/g, consistent with severely increased albuminuria (A3). According to KDIGO CKD classification criteria, the patient should be categorized as G3aA3. Students should utilize the provided eGFR and UACR values to determine CKD stage, albuminuria category, and overall KDIGO risk classification.
The presence of persistent severe albuminuria despite ACE inhibitor and SGLT2 inhibitor therapy identifies the patient as being at very high risk for CKD progression, cardiovascular events, and future kidney failure. According to the 2026 ADA Standards of Care and KDIGO guidelines, albuminuria reduction and preservation of kidney function are major treatment priorities in patients with diabetes and CKD.
Given the patient's progressive albuminuria, established ASCVD, diabetes, and CKD, further optimization of cardiorenal protective therapy represents one of the highest-impact opportunities for risk reduction during today's visit.
2. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus – Uncontrolled
The patient's diabetes remains above goal with an A1C of 8.2%. According to the 2026 ADA Standards of Care, an A1C target of less than 7% is appropriate for many healthy older adults with preserved functional status and reasonable life expectancy. The patient remains above this goal despite treatment with metformin, empagliflozin, and semaglutide.
While glycemic control warrants attention, the primary therapeutic objective in this patient extends beyond glucose lowering alone. Current therapy already includes two medication classes with demonstrated cardiovascular and renal benefit. Therefore, treatment decisions should prioritize therapies that provide both glycemic improvement and reduction in cardiovascular and kidney disease progression risk.
The patient would likely benefit from additional optimization of evidence-based therapy; however, management should be considered within the broader context of cardiorenal risk reduction rather than A1C reduction alone.
3. Hypertension – Above Goal
The patient's office blood pressure is 142/84 mmHg with repeat measurement of 140/82 mmHg. Home blood pressure readings average approximately 138/82 mmHg. According to the 2025 ACC/AHA Hypertension Guideline, the 2026 ADA Standards of Care, and KDIGO recommendations, a blood pressure target of less than 130/80 mmHg is generally recommended for patients with diabetes, CKD, and elevated cardiovascular risk when safely achievable.
Current antihypertensive therapy includes lisinopril and metoprolol succinate. Blood pressure remains above recommended targets, which may contribute to ongoing CKD progression and increased cardiovascular risk. Additional optimization of antihypertensive therapy should be considered; however, prioritization should be guided by overall cardiorenal benefit and the patient's broader risk profile.
4. Secondary ASCVD Prevention – Above Goal
The patient has established ASCVD as evidenced by a prior STEMI requiring PCI with drug-eluting stent placement three years ago. Secondary prevention remains a major therapeutic priority. According to the ADA Standards of Care and ACC/AHA lipid management recommendations, patients with established ASCVD should receive intensive lipid-lowering therapy to reduce future cardiovascular events.
The patient's LDL-C remains elevated at 92 mg/dL despite atorvastatin 40 mg daily. Given her history of myocardial infarction, diabetes, CKD, and persistent LDL elevation, residual cardiovascular risk remains substantial. Additional lipid-lowering strategies may be appropriate following assessment of overall therapeutic priorities.
5. Obesity – Uncontrolled
The patient's BMI is 35.2 kg/m², consistent with Class II obesity. According to the ADA Standards of Care, obesity should be treated as a chronic disease due to its contribution to diabetes progression, hypertension, CKD progression, and cardiovascular risk.
Current physical activity remains limited and dietary habits remain inconsistent. Weight reduction may improve glycemic control, blood pressure, cardiovascular outcomes, and quality of life. Current GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy may provide additional benefit if appropriately optimized.
6. Health Literacy Deficit Regarding CKD and Cardiorenal Protection
The patient demonstrates limited understanding of CKD staging, albuminuria, and cardiorenal protection. She is highly motivated but reports difficulty understanding which conditions should be prioritized. The patient incorrectly views blood glucose as the primary indicator of health status and does not fully appreciate the relationship between albuminuria, kidney disease progression, and cardiovascular risk.
Improved education regarding CKD progression, albuminuria, cardiovascular risk reduction, and medication benefits may improve engagement in long-term disease management.
7. Preventive Care Gap
