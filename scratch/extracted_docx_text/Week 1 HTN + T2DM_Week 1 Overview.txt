Week 1 Overview: Hypertension + Type 2 Diabetes
Why These Disease States Are Paired
Hypertension and type 2 diabetes are among the most common chronic diseases managed in ambulatory care and frequently coexist in the same patient.
Together they significantly increase the risk of:
ASCVD
Stroke
Heart failure
Chronic kidney disease
Mortality
Students must learn to manage both conditions simultaneously rather than in isolation.
Learning Objectives
By the end of Week 1 students should be able to:
Apply hypertension guidelines to patient-specific scenarios.
Apply ADA diabetes recommendations to patient-specific scenarios.
Develop evidence-based treatment plans.
Identify medication therapy problems.
Assess adherence barriers.
Perform patient-centered counseling.
Create complete SOAP notes.
Develop monitoring and follow-up plans.
Guidelines Used
Hypertension:
2025 AHA/ACC Hypertension Guideline
Diabetes:
ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026
Clinical Skills Practiced
Chart review
Patient interviewing
Medication reconciliation
Problem identification
Guideline application
Documentation
Monitoring plan development
Longitudinal Patients
Patient A:
Foundational case focused on uncomplicated hypertension and diabetes management.
Patient B:
Adherence and access case focused on medication barriers, side effects, and practical problem solving.
Patient C:
Higher-risk patient requiring integration of hypertension, diabetes, ASCVD risk reduction, and kidney protection strategies.
Progression of Complexity
Tuesday:
Initial evaluation and treatment planning.
Wednesday:
Assessment of therapeutic response and medication adjustment.
Thursday:
Advanced decision-making involving adherence barriers, side effects, cost considerations, and risk reduction strategies.
The goal is not simply to know the guidelines. The goal is to apply the guidelines to realistic ambulatory care patient encounters.
Weekly Workflow
Monday: Guideline Foundation Day
Students will review:
Hypertension guideline summary
Type 2 diabetes guideline summary
Required readings and resources
The purpose of Monday is to establish the evidence base that will be applied throughout the week.
Tuesday: Initial Patient Assessment
Students will:
Conduct patient interviews
Review chart information
Identify medication therapy problems
Develop initial treatment plans
Complete SOAP note documentation
Wednesday: Follow-Up Assessment
Students will:
Evaluate treatment response
Interpret follow-up laboratory results
Assess adherence and adverse effects
Modify treatment plans as needed
Complete SOAP note documentation
Thursday: Advanced Clinical Decision-Making
Students will:
Manage complex medication therapy problems
Address patient-specific barriers
Apply guideline recommendations to challenging scenarios
Complete SOAP note documentation
Friday: Reflection and Journal Club
Students will:
Reflect on patient care experiences
Apply literature to clinical practice
Participate in journal club discussion
Defend evidence-based treatment recommendations
