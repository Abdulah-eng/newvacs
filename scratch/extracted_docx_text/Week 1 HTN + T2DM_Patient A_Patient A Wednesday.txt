ASSESSMENT
Hypertension – Improved but Above Guideline Goal
The patient's hypertension has improved substantially since the previous visit, with office blood pressure decreasing from 146/92 mmHg to 136/84 mmHg and home readings averaging approximately 134/82 mmHg. Improvement is likely multifactorial and attributable to ACE inhibitor dose escalation, improved adherence, improved blood pressure measurement technique, increased physical activity, and dietary modifications. Despite meaningful progress, blood pressure remains above the guideline-recommended target of less than 130/80 mmHg for patients with diabetes according to the 2025 AHA/ACC Hypertension Guideline.
The patient is tolerating current antihypertensive therapy without adverse effects. Renal function and serum potassium remain stable following lisinopril titration. Given the presence of newly identified albuminuria and persistent blood pressure elevation above goal, further antihypertensive optimization is reasonable. However, because the patient has demonstrated significant improvement over a relatively short interval and residual adherence concerns remain present, either cautious intensification or continued observation with close follow-up may be clinically appropriate.
Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus – Improved but Above Goal
The patient's glycemic control has improved significantly, with A1C decreasing from 8.2% to 7.5% following metformin optimization, improved adherence, dietary changes, increased physical activity, and modest weight loss. Although this represents clinically meaningful improvement, the patient remains above the ADA 2026 recommended A1C target of less than 7% for most nonpregnant adults.
Current therapy is well tolerated and renal function remains adequate for continued metformin use. The patient's obesity, desire for additional weight loss, persistent elevation above glycemic target, and newly identified albuminuria create an opportunity to begin discussing future therapy intensification. While additional observation may be reasonable given recent improvement, initiation of a GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2 inhibitor should now be considered as part of long-term management planning due to their demonstrated cardiovascular, renal, metabolic, and weight-related benefits.
Moderately Increased Albuminuria Consistent with Early Diabetic Kidney Disease Risk
The patient's UACR is 45 mg/g, indicating moderately increased albuminuria. This finding is clinically significant because it identifies early kidney involvement despite preserved eGFR and normal serum creatinine. The presence of albuminuria substantially increases future risk of chronic kidney disease progression and cardiovascular events.
Current ACE inhibitor therapy is appropriate and should be continued. Optimization of blood pressure control becomes increasingly important given the presence of albuminuria. Additionally, this finding strengthens future consideration of therapies with demonstrated renal protection, particularly SGLT2 inhibitors. The patient demonstrates limited understanding of albuminuria and will require education regarding the significance of this finding and its relationship to diabetes and kidney health.
Hyperlipidemia and Primary ASCVD Prevention – Improved
The patient's LDL-C improved from 112 mg/dL to 78 mg/dL following initiation of atorvastatin 20 mg daily. This response is consistent with expected efficacy of moderate-intensity statin therapy and suggests appropriate therapeutic benefit. The patient denies adverse effects and reports generally good adherence, although she admits missing atorvastatin more frequently than her other medications.
Current therapy remains appropriate. Continued adherence should be emphasized because diabetes, hypertension, obesity, albuminuria, and family history collectively place the patient at elevated long-term cardiovascular risk despite the absence of established ASCVD.
Obesity – Improving
The patient has lost approximately 2.5 kg since the previous visit and has successfully implemented several meaningful lifestyle modifications. Weight reduction likely contributed to improvements in blood pressure and glycemic control. Despite this progress, BMI remains within the obesity range and continues to contribute to cardiometabolic risk.
The patient remains highly motivated by weight loss and consistently identifies this as one of her primary health goals. Future treatment decisions should consider therapies that support both glycemic improvement and weight reduction.
Medication Adherence – Improved but Not Yet Optimized
Medication adherence has improved significantly since the previous visit, decreasing from missed doses two to three times weekly to approximately once weekly. While this represents meaningful progress, residual nonadherence remains present and may partially explain persistent elevations in blood pressure and A1C. Continued adherence counseling is warranted before labeling current therapy ineffective.
Preventive Care Gaps
The patient continues to have potential gaps in preventive care, including pneumococcal vaccination, recombinant zoster vaccination, and Tdap booster administration. These recommendations remain appropriate and should continue to be addressed during future visits.
