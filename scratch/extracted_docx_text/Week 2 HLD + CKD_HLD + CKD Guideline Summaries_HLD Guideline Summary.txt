Week 2 Hyperlipidemia & ASCVD Risk Reduction Teaching Summary
Lipid Goals
General Goal
Reduce ASCVD risk through evidence-based LDL-C lowering.
Treatment intensity should be individualized based on:
Presence of ASCVD 
LDL-C level 
CKD status 
Diabetes status 
Overall cardiovascular risk 
Medication tolerance 
Patient preferences 
Initial Evaluation
Assess:
LDL-C 
HDL-C 
Triglycerides 
Non-HDL-C 
ASCVD history 
Diabetes status 
CKD status 
Smoking status 
Blood pressure 
Family history of premature ASCVD 
Current lipid-lowering therapy 
Statin tolerance 
First-Line Therapy
Most patients:
Statin therapy + lifestyle modification
Treatment intensity should be guided by:
Primary versus secondary prevention 
ASCVD risk category 
LDL-C level 
Presence of diabetes 
Presence of CKD 
Statin Therapy
Benefits
LDL-C reduction 
ASCVD event reduction 
Reduction in MI risk 
Reduction in stroke risk 
Reduction in cardiovascular mortality 
High-Intensity Statins
Examples:
Atorvastatin 40–80 mg daily 
Rosuvastatin 20–40 mg daily 
Moderate-Intensity Statins
Examples:
Atorvastatin 10–20 mg daily 
Rosuvastatin 5–10 mg daily 
Simvastatin 20–40 mg daily 
Pravastatin 40–80 mg daily 
Nonstatin Therapy
Ezetimibe
Benefits:
Additional LDL-C lowering 
Oral administration 
Generally well tolerated 
Most commonly used:
When LDL-C remains above goal despite maximally tolerated statin therapy 
When statin intolerance limits therapy 
PCSK9 Inhibitors
Benefits:
Significant LDL-C reduction 
ASCVD risk reduction 
Examples:
Evolocumab 
Alirocumab 
Inclisiran
Benefits:
Significant LDL-C reduction 
Infrequent dosing schedule 
May be considered when additional LDL-C lowering is needed despite optimized therapy.
Hypertriglyceridemia
Assess:
Secondary causes 
Alcohol intake 
Dietary contributors 
Diabetes control 
Medication contributors 
Management:
Lifestyle modification 
Weight reduction 
Glycemic optimization when applicable 
Appropriate pharmacologic therapy based on triglyceride level and cardiovascular risk 
Diabetes and Lipid Management
Patients with diabetes have elevated ASCVD risk.
Assess:
Duration of diabetes 
Additional ASCVD risk enhancers 
CKD status 
Albuminuria 
Most adults with diabetes require statin therapy.
CKD and Lipid Management
CKD increases cardiovascular risk.
Assess:
eGFR 
Albuminuria 
CKD progression risk 
Lipid-lowering therapy remains an important component of cardiovascular risk reduction.

Common Student Mistakes
Focusing only on LDL-C numbers 
Ignoring overall ASCVD risk 
Missing secondary prevention opportunities 
Underutilizing high-intensity statins 
Delaying therapy intensification 
Missing nonstatin therapy indications 
Failing to assess adherence before intensifying therapy 
Ignoring CKD as a cardiovascular risk enhancer 
Escalation Points
Established ASCVD 
Severely elevated LDL-C 
Progressive ASCVD despite therapy 
Statin intolerance limiting treatment 
Persistent LDL-C elevation despite optimized therapy 
Familial hypercholesterolemia concerns 
Very high cardiovascular risk patients
