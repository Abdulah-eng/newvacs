Asthma Overview
Asthma is a chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by variable respiratory symptoms and variable expiratory airflow limitation. Common symptoms include wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough that vary over time and in intensity.
The goals of therapy are to:
Achieve symptom control 
Prevent severe exacerbations 
Preserve lung function 
Minimize medication adverse effects 
Improve quality of life 
A major shift in modern asthma management is the recognition that even patients with infrequent symptoms remain at risk for severe exacerbations. Therefore, GINA recommends ICS-containing therapy for all patients with asthma rather than SABA-only treatment.
Confirming the Diagnosis of Asthma
Whenever possible, objective evidence should be obtained before initiating long-term controller therapy.
Features Supporting Asthma
Symptoms vary over time 
Symptoms worsen at night or early morning 
Symptoms triggered by allergens, exercise, viral infections, weather, smoke, or irritants 
Family history of asthma or atopy 
Spirometry Findings
Diagnostic evidence includes:
Reduced FEV1/FVC ratio 
Bronchodilator response: 
Increase in FEV1 ≥12% 
AND increase ≥200 mL 
Students should recognize that asthma diagnosis requires both:
Compatible symptoms 
Evidence of variable airflow limitation 
Assessment of Asthma Control
Control should be assessed over the previous four weeks.
Well Controlled
Symptoms ≤2 days/week 
No nighttime awakening 
Reliever use ≤2 days/week 
No activity limitation 
Partly Controlled
One to two of the above criteria not met.
Uncontrolled
Three or four criteria not met.
Students should recognize that symptom control and future risk are separate domains.
A patient may have acceptable daily symptoms but remain at high risk for severe exacerbations.
Risk Factors for Future Exacerbations
Important factors include:
Previous severe exacerbation 
Excessive SABA use 
Poor adherence 
Incorrect inhaler technique 
Smoking exposure 
Low lung function 
Elevated eosinophils 
Comorbid conditions

Inhaler Technique and Adherence
Before escalating therapy always evaluate:
Adherence
Missed doses 
Cost barriers 
Misunderstanding of regimen 
Technique
Incorrect inhalation timing 
Poor inspiratory effort 
Failure to prime devices 
Failure to rinse mouth when appropriate 
Poor control is often related to adherence or technique rather than medication failure.
Biologics: Introductory Concepts
Students are not expected to select a biologic.
Students should recognize referral criteria:
Persistent uncontrolled asthma 
Recurrent exacerbations 
High corticosteroid burden 
Evidence of Type 2 inflammation 
Examples include:
Omalizumab 
Mepolizumab 
Benralizumab 
Dupilumab 
Tezepelumab 
Depemokimab 
The pharmacist's role is recognizing appropriate referral rather than independently initiating therapy.
