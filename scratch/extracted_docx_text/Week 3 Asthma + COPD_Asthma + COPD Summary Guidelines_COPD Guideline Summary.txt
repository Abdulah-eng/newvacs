Key Guideline Source
GOLD 2026 Report 
COPD Overview
COPD is characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation caused by airway and/or alveolar abnormalities usually related to exposure to noxious particles or gases.
The goals of therapy are to:
Reduce symptoms 
Improve exercise tolerance 
Prevent exacerbations 
Improve quality of life 
Reduce mortality 
Confirming COPD Diagnosis
Spirometry is required.
Diagnostic criterion:
FEV1/FVC <0.70 after bronchodilator administration
Students should recognize that symptoms alone are insufficient to diagnose COPD.

Patients are classified using:
Symptom Burden

or

Number of moderate exacerbations

GOLD Groups
Group A
Low symptoms Low exacerbation risk
Group B
High symptoms Low exacerbation risk
Group E
Frequent exacerbations
Most treatment decisions focus on identifying Group E patients.
Initial Pharmacologic Therapy
Group A
Single long-acting bronchodilator
Group B
LABA + LAMA
Group E
LABA + LAMA
Consider LABA/LAMA/ICS when:
Blood eosinophils elevated 
Asthma features present 
Blood Eosinophils
Blood eosinophils help estimate likelihood of ICS benefit.
Higher Eosinophils
Greater expected response to ICS-containing regimens.
Lower Eosinophils
Less benefit from ICS.
Students should understand eosinophils as one factor within a broader clinical assessment.
Triple Therapy
Components:
LABA 
LAMA 
ICS 
Consider when:
Continued exacerbations 
Elevated eosinophils 
Appropriate inhaler use confirmed 
Always assess adherence before escalating therapy.

Asthma-COPD Overlap Concepts
Patients with features of both diseases require special consideration.
Students should recognize:
Asthma history 
Reversible obstruction 
Elevated eosinophils 
Smoking-related airflow limitation 
These patients often require ICS-containing therapy and may warrant specialist referral if disease remains uncontrolled.
