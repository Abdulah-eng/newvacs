Week 2 Overview – Hyperlipidemia + Chronic Kidney Disease
Why Hyperlipidemia and CKD Are Paired Together
Hyperlipidemia and CKD are closely linked disease states that significantly increase cardiovascular morbidity and mortality.
Patients with CKD are at substantially increased risk for:
Myocardial infarction 
Stroke 
Heart failure 
Cardiovascular death 
Many patients with CKD are more likely to experience a cardiovascular event than progress to kidney failure.
As kidney disease progresses, cardiovascular risk rises. Likewise, uncontrolled cardiovascular risk factors can accelerate CKD progression.
Modern ambulatory care management requires clinicians to simultaneously address:
Cardiovascular risk reduction 
Kidney protection 
Albuminuria reduction 
Prevention of CKD progression 
Long-term morbidity and mortality reduction 
This week is designed to help students understand how these goals intersect and how treatment decisions often influence multiple disease states simultaneously.

CKD

Interpret UACR results 
Assess CKD progression risk 
Identify patients requiring kidney-protective therapy 
Recognize indications for nephrology referral 
Monitor renal function appropriately 
Cardiorenal Protection
Prioritize therapies that improve both cardiovascular and kidney outcomes

Evaluate ACE inhibitor and ARB optimization 
Identify candidates for SGLT2 inhibitor therapy 
Evaluate finerenone eligibility 
Balance multiple competing disease-state priorities

Week 2 Learning Themes
The following themes should appear repeatedly throughout all patient encounters:
1. Risk Stratification
Students should assess:
ASCVD risk 
CKD progression risk 
Albuminuria burden 
2. Albuminuria Interpretation
Students should learn that:
Albuminuria is a marker of kidney damage 
Albuminuria predicts CKD progression 
Albuminuria predicts cardiovascular risk 
Albuminuria often drives treatment decisions 
3. Statin Optimization
Students should evaluate:
Statin indication 
Statin intensity 
Adherence 
Tolerability 
Need for therapy intensification 
4. Cardiorenal Protection
Students should identify therapies that provide:
Kidney protection 
Cardiovascular protection 
Reduction in future morbidity 
5. CKD Progression Prevention
Students should recognize:
Risk factors for progression 
Opportunities for intervention 
Importance of longitudinal monitoring 
6. Monitoring and Follow-Up
Students should learn:
What to monitor

Comorbidities

Medication tolerance

Students should recognize when:
Therapy should be intensified

Longitudinal Patient Design
Each patient is designed to teach a different aspect of Week 2 content.
Patient A – Foundational CKD Risk Recognition
Clinical Focus
Early-stage CKD 
Mild albuminuria 
Hyperlipidemia requiring intervention 
Missing monitoring data 
Foundational risk assessment 
Student Learning Goals
Identify CKD 
Stage CKD appropriately 
Recognize statin indications 
Develop a monitoring plan 
Establish baseline cardiorenal protection strategies 
This patient serves as the foundational Week 2 case.
Patient B – Adherence and Access Barriers
Clinical Focus
Uncontrolled lipids 
Progressive CKD risk 
Medication nonadherence 
Cost barriers 
Treatment fatigue 
Student Learning Goals
Identify adherence barriers 
Address affordability concerns 
Avoid unnecessary therapy escalation 
Develop practical interventions 
Apply patient-centered communication 
This patient teaches students that uncontrolled disease is not always caused by inadequate therapy.
Patient C – High-Risk Cardiorenal Protection
Clinical Focus
Advanced CKD risk 
Significant albuminuria 
Diabetes 
Elevated ASCVD risk 
Multiple evidence-based therapy opportunities 
Student Learning Goals
Integrate multiple guidelines 
Prioritize therapies appropriately 
Optimize cardiorenal protection 
Develop comprehensive monitoring plans 
Manage complex patients longitudinally 
This patient serves as the most advanced Week 2 case.
