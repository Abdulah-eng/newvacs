The observed LDL-C reduction suggests a favorable response to therapy and supports continued adherence to the current regimen.

The patient continues to meet criteria for CKD G2A2 based on preserved eGFR (78 mL/min/1.73m²) and persistent moderately increased albuminuria (UACR 102 mg/g). Kidney function remains stable compared with the previous visit, and albuminuria demonstrates modest improvement.
According to the KDIGO 2024 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Evaluation and Management of Chronic Kidney Disease, CKD classification should incorporate both eGFR and albuminuria because each independently predicts kidney disease progression and cardiovascular risk. Persistent A2 albuminuria continues to represent evidence of kidney damage despite preserved filtration function.
The patient's improved blood pressure control, improved medication adherence, and reduced NSAID exposure are encouraging. However, persistent albuminuria indicates residual CKD progression risk and warrants continued monitoring and kidney-protective interventions.

Blood pressure has improved from approximately 136/84 mmHg at the previous visit to 132/80 mmHg today with supportive home readings averaging 131/79 mmHg. The patient reports improved adherence and increased lifestyle modification efforts.
According to the KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline, blood pressure control is one of the most important interventions for slowing CKD progression and reducing cardiovascular risk. Improved blood pressure likely contributes to both cardiovascular risk reduction and kidney protection.
Current therapy appears effective and well tolerated. Continued monitoring and reinforcement of lifestyle modifications are appropriate.
4. Medication Adherence – Improved
The patient reports substantial improvement in adherence after implementing behavioral strategies. Missed doses decreased from approximately two to three doses weekly to approximately one missed dose every two weeks.
Medication adherence likely contributed significantly to improvement in LDL-C and blood pressure control.
Continued reinforcement of successful adherence strategies is appropriate.
