Week 3 Overview – Asthma + Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)
Why Asthma and COPD Are Paired Together
Asthma and COPD are among the most common chronic respiratory diseases encountered in ambulatory care practice.
Although traditionally taught as separate disease states, many patients demonstrate overlapping characteristics that blur the distinction between asthma and COPD. These patients often present with:
• Persistent respiratory symptoms
• Airflow limitation
• Recurrent exacerbations
• Frequent healthcare utilization
• Reduced quality of life
Both asthma and COPD are associated with significant morbidity, and exacerbations contribute to:
• Emergency department visits
• Hospitalizations
• Accelerated lung function decline
• Increased healthcare costs
• Increased mortality
Modern ambulatory care management focuses on more than symptom control. Clinicians must simultaneously address:
• Exacerbation prevention
• Optimization of inhaler therapy
• Medication adherence
• Inhaler technique
• Airway inflammation
• Comorbid disease states
• Long-term disease progression
• Patient quality of life
This week is designed to help students understand how pulmonary disease management extends beyond simply prescribing inhalers and requires ongoing reassessment, monitoring, and individualized therapeutic decision-making.
What Students Should Learn This Week
By the end of Week 3, students should be able to:
Asthma
• Assess asthma symptom control
• Interpret Asthma Control Test (ACT) scores
• Apply GINA 2025 treatment recommendations
• Select appropriate inhaled corticosteroid therapy
• Implement SMART therapy
• Assess rescue inhaler utilization
• Evaluate exacerbation risk
• Assess inhaler technique
• Monitor treatment response
COPD
• Assess COPD symptom burden
• Interpret COPD Assessment Test (CAT) scores
• Apply GOLD 2026 treatment recommendations
• Select appropriate bronchodilator therapy
• Identify candidates for LABA/LAMA therapy
• Recognize indications for triple therapy
• Interpret blood eosinophil counts
• Evaluate exacerbation risk
• Monitor COPD treatment response
Asthma-COPD Overlap and Severe Airway Disease
• Recognize overlapping asthma and COPD features
• Interpret bronchodilator reversibility
• Identify eosinophilic airway inflammation
• Evaluate candidates for biologic therapy
• Apply evidence from emerging clinical trials
• Develop advanced longitudinal management strategies

Clinical Data Interpretation
