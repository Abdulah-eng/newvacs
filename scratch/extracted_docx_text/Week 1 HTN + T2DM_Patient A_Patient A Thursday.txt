ASSESSMENT
Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus – Significantly Improved but Above Individualized Goal
The patient's glycemic control has improved substantially over the past six months, with A1C decreasing from 8.2% to 7.5% and now 7.1%. Improvement is likely attributable to enhanced medication adherence, metformin optimization, weight loss, dietary modification, and increased physical activity. Despite this progress, the patient remains slightly above the ADA 2026 recommended A1C target of less than 7% for most nonpregnant adults.
Importantly, treatment decisions at this stage should extend beyond A1C reduction alone. The patient has obesity, moderately increased albuminuria, hypertension, and multiple cardiovascular risk factors. Therefore, future therapy intensification should prioritize therapies capable of providing cardiovascular, renal, and weight-management benefits in addition to glycemic improvement. The patient appears to be an excellent candidate for GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy and may also derive benefit from SGLT2 inhibitor therapy depending on future treatment priorities and patient preferences.
Moderately Increased Albuminuria and Elevated Risk for Diabetic Kidney Disease Progression
The patient's UACR remains elevated at 38 mg/g despite modest improvement from 45 mg/g previously. Persistent albuminuria indicates ongoing renal injury and remains an important predictor of future chronic kidney disease progression and cardiovascular events. Preservation of eGFR and serum creatinine should not be interpreted as absence of kidney disease risk.
Current ACE inhibitor therapy is appropriate and blood pressure is now controlled. However, persistent albuminuria supports consideration of additional renoprotective interventions. Based on ADA 2026 recommendations, an SGLT2 inhibitor may provide meaningful renal and cardiovascular benefit independent of glycemic control.
Hypertension – Controlled
The patient's blood pressure is now controlled with office measurements of 128/78 mmHg and home readings averaging approximately 126/77 mmHg. Significant improvement has occurred since baseline and likely reflects successful medication optimization, adherence improvement, weight loss, and lifestyle modification. Current therapy is well tolerated without evidence of hypotension, renal dysfunction, or hyperkalemia. No additional antihypertensive intensification is indicated at this time.
Hyperlipidemia and Primary ASCVD Prevention – Controlled
LDL-C has improved from 112 mg/dL at baseline to 70 mg/dL on atorvastatin 20 mg daily. Current therapy is well tolerated and appears effective. Despite the absence of established ASCVD, the patient's diabetes, hypertension, obesity, albuminuria, and family history continue to place her at elevated lifetime cardiovascular risk. Ongoing statin therapy remains appropriate.
Obesity – Improving but Persistent
The patient has achieved meaningful weight loss of approximately 5.5 kg since initiation of ambulatory care management. Lifestyle modifications have been successful and should be reinforced. Despite this progress, obesity remains present and continues to contribute to cardiometabolic risk. The patient remains highly motivated to pursue additional weight loss and would likely benefit from therapies with demonstrated weight reduction efficacy.
Preventive Care Gaps
The patient remains overdue for several guideline-recommended immunizations, including pneumococcal vaccination, recombinant zoster vaccination, and Tdap booster administration. These preventive interventions remain important components of comprehensive chronic disease management.
