ASSESSMENT
1. Hyperlipidemia / Elevated ASCVD Risk – Uncontrolled
The patient has uncontrolled hyperlipidemia with an LDL-C of 148 mg/dL while receiving low-intensity statin therapy with simvastatin 10 mg daily. She possesses multiple cardiovascular risk factors including CKD, hypertension, obesity, advanced age, and family history of ASCVD. Her estimated 10-year ASCVD risk is 17.4%.
According to the 2026 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Management of Blood Cholesterol, CKD is a risk-enhancing condition that should be incorporated into cardiovascular risk assessment and treatment decisions. The patient's current statin regimen is unlikely to provide adequate LDL-C reduction given her risk profile.
Importantly, apparent treatment failure is not due to lack of efficacy but rather failure to initiate previously prescribed guideline-directed therapy. Rosuvastatin 20 mg daily was prescribed but never started.
2. Therapeutic Inertia Secondary to Medication Noninitiation
The patient was prescribed rosuvastatin 20 mg daily approximately six months ago but never initiated treatment.
The primary contributors to noninitiation include:
Medication affordability concerns 
Fear of adverse effects 
Lack of communication with providers regarding barriers 
This case illustrates therapeutic inertia resulting from unresolved patient-specific barriers rather than inappropriate prescribing.
Successful management requires addressing barriers before considering additional medication changes.
3. Chronic Kidney Disease (G3aA2) – Stable but At Elevated Risk
The patient has CKD G3aA2 based on eGFR of 54 mL/min/1.73m² and persistent moderately increased albuminuria (UACR 165 mg/g).
According to the KDIGO 2024 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Evaluation and Management of Chronic Kidney Disease, both GFR and albuminuria should be incorporated into CKD staging because both independently predict future kidney disease progression and cardiovascular risk.
Persistent albuminuria and reduced eGFR place the patient at elevated risk for both kidney disease progression and cardiovascular events.
Continued risk reduction remains essential.
4. Hypertension – Above Goal
Blood pressure remains elevated with office readings approximately 140/85 mmHg and similar home values.
According to KDIGO 2024, blood pressure control remains one of the most important interventions for reducing CKD progression risk and cardiovascular complications.
Additional assessment of adherence, home monitoring practices, and lifestyle contributors is warranted before considering medication intensification.
5. Medication Affordability Barrier
The patient reports concerns regarding medication costs and has occasionally altered medication-taking behavior because of affordability concerns.
Financial barriers are contributing to medication-related decision-making and represent a significant threat to long-term disease control.
Additional assessment of insurance coverage, pharmacy costs, and cost-saving resources is warranted.
6. Fear of Medication Adverse Effects
The patient reports concern regarding statin-associated adverse effects after hearing anecdotal reports from friends.
These concerns contributed directly to failure to initiate rosuvastatin therapy.
Patient education regarding actual benefits and risks of therapy is warranted to support informed shared decision-making.
7. Obesity
BMI is 36.3 kg/m² and likely contributes to elevated cardiovascular and kidney disease risk.
Both the 2026 Hyperlipidemia Guideline and KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline emphasize lifestyle modification and weight management as important components of long-term cardiovascular and kidney risk reduction.
Lifestyle intervention should be incorporated into the treatment plan.
