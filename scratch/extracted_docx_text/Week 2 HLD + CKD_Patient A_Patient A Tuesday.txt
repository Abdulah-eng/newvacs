ASSESSMENT
1. Hyperlipidemia / Elevated ASCVD Risk – Suboptimally Controlled
The patient has hyperlipidemia with an LDL-C of 132 mg/dL despite atorvastatin 10 mg daily. He is a 58-year-old male with hypertension, CKD, obesity, former tobacco use, and a family history of premature ASCVD. His estimated 10-year ASCVD risk is 11.8%, placing him at elevated cardiovascular risk.
According to the 2026 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Management of Blood Cholesterol, CKD is considered an ASCVD risk-enhancing condition and should be incorporated into treatment decisions for primary prevention. Patients with elevated cardiovascular risk should receive appropriately intensive LDL-C lowering therapy to reduce future ASCVD events. The patient's LDL-C remains above desirable treatment thresholds and he possesses multiple risk-enhancing factors that support statin intensification.
Suboptimal LDL-C control is likely multifactorial and related to both inadequate statin intensity and medication nonadherence. The patient reports missing atorvastatin approximately two to three times weekly. Although adherence counseling is warranted, persistent LDL-C elevation and overall cardiovascular risk support escalation to a moderate-intensity statin regimen at this visit.
2. Chronic Kidney Disease (G2A2) – Stable but At Increased Risk for Progression
The patient has CKD stage G2A2 based on an eGFR of 79 mL/min/1.73m² and persistent moderately increased albuminuria with UACR values of 118 mg/g and 105 mg/g on repeat testing. Although kidney filtration remains preserved, persistent albuminuria confirms ongoing kidney damage.
According to the KDIGO 2024 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Evaluation and Management of Chronic Kidney Disease, CKD should be classified using both GFR and albuminuria categories because both independently predict kidney disease progression and cardiovascular risk. Persistent A2 albuminuria represents clinically significant kidney damage even in the setting of preserved eGFR.
Current kidney function appears stable; however, persistent albuminuria, hypertension, obesity, and chronic NSAID exposure increase long-term risk for CKD progression. Continued ACE inhibitor therapy, cardiovascular risk reduction, avoidance of nephrotoxins, and ongoing monitoring remain important components of management.
3. Hypertension – Near Goal
The patient's blood pressure today was 136/84 mmHg with repeat measurement of 134/82 mmHg. Home blood pressure readings appear consistent with office measurements. Current therapy consists of lisinopril and hydrochlorothiazide, both of which are tolerated without adverse effects. Renal function and serum potassium remain stable.
According to the KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline, blood pressure control remains one of the most effective interventions for slowing CKD progression and reducing cardiovascular risk. Persistent elevations in blood pressure contribute to worsening albuminuria, accelerated kidney function decline, and increased ASCVD risk.
Although blood pressure control is improved compared with many uncontrolled hypertensive patients, readings remain slightly above ideal treatment targets for patients with CKD. Given stable kidney function and the absence of severe hypertension, continued monitoring, lifestyle modification, and adherence reinforcement are appropriate before additional pharmacologic intensification is considered.
4. Chronic NSAID Use – Potentially Harmful
The patient reports using ibuprofen several times weekly for chronic knee pain. He was unaware that NSAIDs may negatively affect kidney health and blood pressure control.
According to the KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline, avoidance of potentially nephrotoxic medications is an important component of CKD management and progression prevention. Chronic NSAID exposure may contribute to worsening blood pressure control, kidney injury, and future CKD progression.
Patient education regarding safer pain-management strategies and minimization of NSAID exposure is warranted.
5. Obesity
The patient's BMI of 32.6 kg/m² is consistent with obesity and contributes to elevated cardiovascular risk, hypertension, and future CKD progression risk. The patient identifies weight loss as one of his primary health goals and appears motivated to make lifestyle changes.
Both the 2026 Hyperlipidemia Guideline and KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline emphasize lifestyle modification and weight management as important components of long-term cardiovascular and kidney risk reduction. Even modest weight reduction may improve overall cardiometabolic health and support achievement of treatment goals.
The patient's motivation to lose weight creates an opportunity for successful implementation of dietary and physical activity interventions.
6. Preventive Care Gaps
The patient appears to have several preventive care gaps, including lack of documented pneumococcal vaccination, herpes zoster vaccination, and an overdue Tdap booster.
Patients with CKD and elevated cardiovascular risk frequently benefit from comprehensive preventive care strategies in addition to disease-specific pharmacotherapy. Immunization review and optimization should be incorporated into routine ambulatory care management to reduce preventable complications.
Further verification of immunization history is warranted.
