1. Severe Eosinophilic Asthma-COPD Overlap with Persistent Exacerbations Despite Optimized Inhaled Therapy
The patient has asthma-COPD overlap characterized by longstanding asthma, persistent airflow obstruction, elevated eosinophils, allergic disease, and recurrent exacerbations. Since initiation of ICS-containing therapy, she has experienced meaningful improvement in baseline symptom control, demonstrating that her current inhaled regimen is effective. However, despite excellent adherence, appropriate inhaler technique, and optimization of comorbid allergic rhinitis, she continues to experience frequent corticosteroid-treated exacerbations.
According to the 2025 GINA Report, patients who continue to experience exacerbations despite high-intensity inhaled therapy should be evaluated for severe asthma and consideration of biologic therapy. The patient's persistent exacerbation burden despite appropriate treatment suggests ongoing uncontrolled Type 2 airway inflammation that is not adequately controlled with inhaled therapy alone.
Her current presentation is consistent with severe eosinophilic airway disease requiring consideration of advanced therapy.
2. Recurrent Corticosteroid-Treated Exacerbations
The patient has experienced three moderate exacerbations during the previous three months and five corticosteroid-treated exacerbations during the past year. According to both GINA 2025 and GOLD 2026, exacerbation history remains one of the strongest predictors of future exacerbation risk and should be a major driver of treatment decisions.
Repeated exacerbations are associated with accelerated decline in lung function, reduced quality of life, increased healthcare utilization, and increased future hospitalization risk. Despite appropriate inhaled therapy, exacerbation frequency remains unacceptably high and warrants therapeutic escalation.
3. Eosinophilic Airway Inflammation
The patient's absolute eosinophil count is 520 cells/µL, indicating persistent eosinophilic inflammation despite optimized inhaled therapy. Elevated eosinophil counts are associated with increased exacerbation risk and represent an important treatable trait in severe asthma management.
The recently published MATINEE trial demonstrated that mepolizumab reduced clinically significant exacerbations among patients with COPD characterized by an eosinophilic phenotype who continued to exacerbate despite optimized inhaled therapy. While this patient has asthma-COPD overlap rather than isolated COPD, the presence of severe eosinophilic disease, recurrent exacerbations, and optimized inhaled therapy makes mepolizumab an appropriate therapeutic consideration.
4. Excessive Systemic Corticosteroid Exposure
The patient has required multiple courses of prednisone during the previous year. She reports insomnia and jitteriness associated with corticosteroid use and has a history of osteopenia, placing her at increased risk for long-term corticosteroid-related complications.
According to GINA 2025, minimizing systemic corticosteroid exposure is a major treatment goal in patients with severe asthma due to the significant burden of cumulative adverse effects. Additional therapies capable of reducing exacerbations and future corticosteroid exposure should be prioritized.
5. Allergic Rhinitis – Controlled
The patient reports significant improvement in allergic rhinitis symptoms following resumption of fluticasone nasal spray. Allergic rhinitis no longer appears to be a major contributor to current respiratory symptoms.
Continued management remains appropriate.
6. Osteopenia
The patient has a history of osteopenia and remains at increased risk for adverse skeletal outcomes associated with repeated systemic corticosteroid exposure. Reducing future exacerbations and minimizing prednisone use may help reduce long-term skeletal risk.
