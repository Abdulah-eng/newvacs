Week 2 Cardiorenal Protection Integration Guide
Primary Learning Objective
Students should learn to prioritize therapies that simultaneously:
Reduce cardiovascular risk 
Slow CKD progression 
Reduce albuminuria 
Prevent future kidney failure 
Improve long-term patient outcomes 
Management decisions should not be based on a single disease state in isolation.
Core Principle #1
CKD Is a Cardiovascular Disease Risk Multiplier
Patients with CKD are more likely to experience:
Myocardial infarction 
Stroke 
Heart failure 
Cardiovascular death 
Many CKD patients will experience a cardiovascular event before progressing to kidney failure.
Clinical implication:
Cardiovascular risk reduction should occur alongside kidney protection.
Core Principle #2
Albuminuria Matters as Much as eGFR
Students frequently focus on eGFR and overlook albuminuria.
Albuminuria is:
A marker of kidney damage 
A marker of CKD progression risk 
A marker of cardiovascular risk 
Always evaluate:
eGFR 
UACR 
Together rather than independently.
Core Principle #3
Therapy Selection Should Extend Beyond A1c
Historically, diabetes management focused primarily on glycemic control.
Modern management prioritizes:
Kidney protection 
Cardiovascular protection 
Heart failure prevention 
Risk reduction 
A patient can have:
A1c at goal 
Progressive albuminuria 
Worsening CKD 
and still require treatment intensification.
Cardiorenal Protection Framework
When evaluating a patient with CKD and cardiovascular risk, assess the following:
Step 1
Is the patient receiving appropriate lipid-lowering therapy?
Assess:
ASCVD history 
Diabetes status 
CKD status 
LDL-C 
Potential interventions:
Initiate statin 
Intensify statin 
Add ezetimibe 
Consider PCSK9-directed therapy 
Step 2
Is blood pressure optimized?
Assess:
Current blood pressure 
Home blood pressure readings 
Medication adherence 
Albuminuria 
Potential interventions:
Optimize ACE inhibitor 
Optimize ARB 
Intensify antihypertensive therapy 
Step 3
Is kidney-protective therapy optimized?
Assess:
eGFR 
UACR 
Diabetes status 
Potential interventions:
Initiate SGLT2 inhibitor 
Optimize ACEi/ARB therapy 
Evaluate finerenone eligibility 
Step 4
Is cardiovascular risk being addressed?
Assess:
ASCVD history 
Smoking status 
Lipid control 
Blood pressure control 
Diabetes status 
Potential interventions:
Lifestyle modification 
Statin optimization 
Tobacco cessation 
Weight management 
Medication Prioritization
ACE Inhibitor / ARB
Primary goals:
Reduce albuminuria 
Slow CKD progression 
Improve cardiovascular outcomes 
Monitor:
Potassium 
Serum creatinine 
Blood pressure 
SGLT2 Inhibitor
Primary goals:
Slow CKD progression 
Reduce heart failure risk 
Reduce cardiovascular events 
Monitor:
eGFR 
Volume status 
Adverse effects 
Finerenone
Primary goals:
Reduce albuminuria 
Slow CKD progression 
Reduce cardiovascular risk 
Monitor:
Potassium 
Renal function 
Statin Therapy
Primary goals:
Reduce ASCVD risk 
Reduce cardiovascular events 
Monitor:
Lipid panel 
Adherence 
Tolerability 
Clinical Decision-Making Scenarios
Scenario 1
Patient:
T2DM 
eGFR 58 mL/min/1.73m² 
UACR 450 mg/g 
LDL-C 132 mg/dL 
Clinical priorities:
Statin therapy optimization 
ACEi/ARB optimization 
SGLT2 inhibitor evaluation 
Albuminuria reduction 
Cardiovascular risk reduction 
Scenario 2
Patient:
LDL-C at goal 
Progressive albuminuria 
Worsening CKD 
Clinical priorities:
Review ACEi/ARB therapy 
Evaluate SGLT2 inhibitor use 
Assess finerenone eligibility 
Assess adherence 
Intensify kidney-protective therapy 
Do not assume management is optimized simply because LDL-C is controlled.
Scenario 3
Patient:
A1c 6.8% 
eGFR declining 
Persistent albuminuria 
Clinical priorities:
Kidney protection 
CKD progression prevention 
Albuminuria reduction 
Do not focus exclusively on glycemic control.
Scenario 4
Patient:
CKD 
Diabetes 
ASCVD history 
Clinical priorities:
High-intensity statin therapy 
ACEi/ARB optimization 
SGLT2 inhibitor evaluation 
Lifestyle modification 
Ongoing monitoring 
These patients frequently qualify for multiple evidence-based therapies simultaneously.
Monitoring Priorities
Assess routinely:
eGFR 
UACR 
Blood pressure 
LDL-C 
Medication adherence 
Potassium 
Serum creatinine 
ASCVD risk factors 
Trend data over time rather than relying on a single value.
Pharmacist-Specific Interventions
Identify untreated albuminuria 
Optimize statin therapy 
Optimize ACEi/ARB therapy 
Identify SGLT2 inhibitor opportunities 
Evaluate finerenone eligibility 
Assess adherence barriers 
Review affordability concerns 
Perform renal dose adjustment review 
Develop monitoring plans 
Provide patient-centered counseling 
Common Student Mistakes
Treating CKD and ASCVD as separate disease states 
Focusing only on LDL-C 
Focusing only on eGFR 
Ignoring albuminuria 
Focusing only on A1c 
Missing opportunities for cardiorenal-protective therapy 
Not assessing medication adherence 
Delaying therapy intensification 
Failing to prioritize highest-risk problems first 
Escalation Points
Progressive CKD despite therapy 
Persistent severe albuminuria 
Rapid eGFR decline 
Recurrent hyperkalemia 
Refractory hypertension 
Established ASCVD with uncontrolled risk factors 
Failure to achieve risk-reduction goals 
Need for nephrology referral 
Need for advanced lipid-lowering therapy
