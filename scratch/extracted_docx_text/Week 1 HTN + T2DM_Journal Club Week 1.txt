Journal Club Key
FLOW Trial: Effects of Semaglutide on Chronic Kidney Disease in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes
Perkovic V, Tuttle KR, Rossing P, et al. Effects of Semaglutide on Chronic Kidney Disease in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes. N Engl J Med. 2024;390:1097-1111.
Background
• Chronic kidney disease (CKD) affects approximately 40% of patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and is associated with increased risks of kidney failure, cardiovascular disease, hospitalization, and death.
• SGLT2 inhibitors have become foundational therapy for diabetic kidney disease; however, substantial residual renal and cardiovascular risk remains despite optimized treatment.
• GLP-1 receptor agonists have demonstrated cardiovascular benefits in previous cardiovascular outcomes trials, but definitive renal outcomes data were limited.
• The FLOW trial was designed to determine whether semaglutide reduces kidney disease progression and cardiovascular outcomes in patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD.
Study Objective
To determine whether semaglutide reduces the risk of major kidney disease events, cardiovascular events, and death in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease.
Methods
Study Design
• International
• Multicenter
• Randomized
• Double-blind
• Placebo-controlled trial
Funding Source
• Novo Nordisk
Patient Population
• 3,533 patients enrolled
• Semaglutide group: n = 1,767
• Placebo group: n = 1,766
Major Inclusion Criteria
• Age ≥18 years
• Type 2 diabetes mellitus
• CKD with:
eGFR 50–75 mL/min/1.73m² with UACR 300–5000 mg/g 
OR
eGFR 25–50 mL/min/1.73m² with UACR 100–5000 mg/g 
• Receiving standard-of-care therapy including RAAS blockade when appropriate
Major Exclusion Criteria
• Type 1 diabetes mellitus
• Kidney transplant recipient
• Dialysis-dependent kidney disease
• Recent use of GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy
• Recent major cardiovascular event
Drug Treatment
Treatment Group
• Semaglutide 1 mg subcutaneous injection weekly
Placebo Group
• Matching placebo injection weekly
Background Therapy
Patients continued guideline-directed therapy including:
• ACE inhibitor or ARB
• SGLT2 inhibitor when appropriate
• Statin therapy
• Antihypertensive therapy
Primary Endpoint
Composite of:
• Kidney failure
• Sustained ≥50% decline in eGFR
• Death from kidney causes
• Death from cardiovascular causes
Secondary Endpoints
• Major kidney outcomes
• Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE)
• All-cause mortality
• Rate of eGFR decline
Safety Outcomes
• Serious adverse events
• Acute kidney injury
• Severe hypoglycemia
• Pancreatitis
• Gastrointestinal adverse effects
• Gallbladder-related events
Statistics
• Intention-to-treat analysis
• Cox proportional hazards models used
• Two-sided alpha = 0.05
• Trial stopped early due to demonstrated efficacy
Data Collection / Frequency / Assessment Tools
• Scheduled follow-up visits throughout trial
• Median follow-up approximately 3.4 years
• Laboratory monitoring included:
eGFR 
Serum creatinine 
UACR 
• Outcomes independently adjudicated

Author's Conclusion
"Among patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, semaglutide reduced the risk of major kidney disease events, cardiovascular events, and death compared with placebo."
Strengths
• First dedicated renal outcomes trial for a GLP-1 receptor agonist
• Large international randomized controlled trial
• Clinically meaningful kidney outcomes
• Included high-risk CKD population
• Modern background therapy
• Strong statistical power
• Demonstrated both renal and cardiovascular benefit
Limitations
• Trial stopped early due to efficacy
• Limited long-term follow-up beyond study period
• Injectable medication may affect real-world adherence
• Predominantly albuminuric CKD population limits generalizability to lower-risk CKD
• Industry funded
Presenter's Conclusion / Clinical Application
FLOW is one of the most important nephrology and diabetes trials published in the last several years. The study demonstrated that semaglutide provides clinically meaningful renal and cardiovascular protection in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease.
The trial supports the growing shift away from viewing GLP-1 receptor agonists solely as glucose-lowering medications. Instead, semaglutide should be considered a cardiorenal risk-reduction therapy in appropriately selected patients with diabetes and CKD.
For pharmacists, FLOW reinforces the importance of evaluating:
• CKD stage
• Albuminuria
• ASCVD history
• Residual cardiorenal risk
when selecting therapy rather than focusing exclusively on A1C reduction.
Patients most likely to benefit include those with:
• Type 2 diabetes
• CKD
• Albuminuria
• Established ASCVD
• Persistent residual risk despite ACE inhibitor/ARB and SGLT2 inhibitor therapy

Five Clinical Application Questions
1. A patient with T2DM, eGFR 48 mL/min/1.73m², UACR 520 mg/g, and A1C 7.3% is already taking metformin and empagliflozin. Would FLOW support adding semaglutide?
Answer: Yes. The patient's significant albuminuria and CKD place them at high cardiorenal risk. FLOW demonstrated kidney and cardiovascular benefits beyond glucose lowering.
2. How would you explain semaglutide's benefit to a patient whose A1C is already controlled?
Answer: Semaglutide helps reduce future kidney disease progression and cardiovascular events, not just blood sugar.
3. A patient with prior MI, CKD Stage 3a, and UACR 450 mg/g is already taking an ACE inhibitor, SGLT2 inhibitor, and statin. Should semaglutide be considered?
Answer: Yes. This patient resembles the high-risk population studied in FLOW and has substantial residual cardiovascular and renal risk.
4. A patient's eGFR declines from 55 to 49 mL/min/1.73m² over six months while taking semaglutide. Does this indicate treatment failure?
Answer: No. CKD progression should be assessed using overall trends, albuminuria changes, and clinical outcomes rather than a single eGFR value.
5. Which patient would likely derive greater benefit from semaglutide?
Patient A:
A1C 9.5% 
No CKD 
No ASCVD 
Patient B:
A1C 7.2% 
CKD Stage 3a 
UACR 700 mg/g 
Prior MI 
Answer: Patient B. FLOW supports selecting therapy based on cardiorenal risk reduction rather than A1C alone.
