Week 2 Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) Guideline Teaching Summary
CKD Goals
General Goal
Slow CKD progression, reduce cardiovascular risk, prevent kidney failure, and minimize CKD-related complications.
Goals should be individualized based on:
eGFR 
Albuminuria level 
Cause of CKD 
Rate of progression 
Cardiovascular risk 
Diabetes status 
Blood pressure control 
Life expectancy 
Patient preferences 
Initial Evaluation
Assess:
eGFR 
UACR 
Blood pressure 
Cause of CKD 
Diabetes status 
ASCVD history 
Heart failure history 
Current medications 
Nephrotoxic medication use 
Electrolytes 
Weight 
Tobacco use 
Confirm:
Kidney abnormalities have been present for ≥3 months 
Persistent albuminuria when present 
CKD stage using both eGFR and albuminuria 
CKD Classification
GFR Categories
G1: eGFR ≥90 mL/min/1.73m² 
G2: eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73m² 
G3a: eGFR 45–59 mL/min/1.73m² 
G3b: eGFR 30–44 mL/min/1.73m² 
G4: eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73m² 
G5: eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73m² 
Albuminuria Categories
A1: UACR <30 mg/g 
A2: UACR 30–300 mg/g 
A3: UACR >300 mg/g 
Both eGFR and albuminuria should be used when assessing CKD progression risk. 
First-Line Therapy
Most patients:
Lifestyle modification + blood pressure optimization + kidney-protective pharmacotherapy
Therapy selection should be guided by:
Albuminuria status 
Diabetes status 
eGFR 
Cardiovascular risk 
Heart failure status 
Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibitors (ACEi/ARB)
Benefits
Reduce albuminuria 
Slow CKD progression 
Lower cardiovascular risk 
Improve kidney outcomes 
Recommended for many patients with:
CKD 
Albuminuria 
Hypertension 
Examples:
Lisinopril 
Enalapril 
Losartan 
Valsartan 
Monitoring:
Serum creatinine 
Potassium 
Blood pressure 
UACR 
SGLT2 Inhibitors
Benefits
Slow CKD progression 
Reduce albuminuria 
Reduce heart failure events 
Reduce cardiovascular risk 
Examples:
Empagliflozin 
Dapagliflozin 
Use should be based on guideline-recommended eGFR thresholds and clinical indication.
SGLT2 inhibitor therapy should be considered for many patients with:
CKD 
Albuminuria 
Type 2 diabetes 
Increased cardiorenal risk 
Nonsteroidal Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists
Benefits
Reduce albuminuria 
Slow CKD progression 
Reduce cardiovascular events 
Example:
Finerenone 
May be considered in appropriate patients with:
Type 2 diabetes 
CKD 
Persistent albuminuria despite optimized therapy 
Monitoring:
Potassium 
Renal function 
Blood Pressure Management
Blood pressure control is one of the most important interventions for slowing CKD progression.
Assess:
Office blood pressure 
Home blood pressure readings when available 
Adherence 
Lifestyle factors 
Persistent uncontrolled blood pressure accelerates kidney disease progression and increases cardiovascular risk.
Medication Stewardship
Review for:
NSAID use 
Duplicate therapy 
Inappropriate dosing 
Nephrotoxic medications 
Herbal supplements 
OTC medications 
Dose adjustments may be required as kidney function declines.
Diabetic Kidney Disease
Patients with diabetes should routinely undergo:
eGFR monitoring 
UACR monitoring 
Presence of albuminuria should influence treatment decisions independent of A1c goals.
Management should focus on:
Kidney protection 
Cardiovascular risk reduction 
Blood pressure optimization 
Glucose management 
Monitoring
eGFR 
UACR 
Blood pressure 
Potassium 
Serum creatinine 
Medication adherence 
Medication tolerability 
CKD progression 
Cardiovascular risk factors 
Monitoring frequency should increase as CKD severity progresses.
Lifestyle Counseling
Sodium reduction 
Weight management 
Physical activity 
Tobacco cessation 
Medication adherence 
Avoidance of nephrotoxic medications 
Cardiovascular risk reduction 
Pharmacist-Specific Interventions
CKD staging verification 
Albuminuria assessment 
ACEi/ARB optimization 
SGLT2 inhibitor optimization 
Finerenone evaluation 
Renal dose adjustment review 
Medication reconciliation 
Adherence assessment 
Monitoring plan development 
Patient education regarding kidney protection 
Common Student Mistakes
Focusing only on eGFR 
Ignoring albuminuria 
Missing CKD diagnosis in early stages 
Delaying initiation of kidney-protective therapies 
Discontinuing ACEi/ARB therapy unnecessarily 
Failing to assess nephrotoxic medication use 
Missing indications for SGLT2 inhibitor therapy 
Ignoring cardiovascular risk in CKD patients 
Not monitoring potassium appropriately 
Failing to trend kidney function over time 
Escalation Points
Rapid eGFR decline 
Progressive albuminuria 
Persistent A3 albuminuria 
Refractory hypertension 
Recurrent hyperkalemia 
Significant medication-related adverse effects 
Suspected secondary kidney disease 
Advanced CKD approaching kidney failure 
Need for nephrology referral 
Evidence of accelerated CKD progression
