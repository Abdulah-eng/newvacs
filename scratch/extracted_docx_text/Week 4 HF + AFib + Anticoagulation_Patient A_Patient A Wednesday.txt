Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF) – Improving on Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy, Further Optimization Indicated
Michael Thompson is a 58-year-old male with symptomatic HFrEF (LVEF 35%) who presents for follow-up approximately 3 months after initiation of guideline-directed medical therapy. Since the previous visit, he has experienced significant clinical improvement as evidenced by reduced dyspnea, improved exercise tolerance, increased physical activity, resolution of lower extremity edema, and improved energy levels. Objective improvement is also demonstrated by a reduction in BNP from 320 pg/mL to 165 pg/mL and a modest decrease in body weight. The patient remains mildly symptomatic with more strenuous exertion, consistent with persistent NYHA Class II heart failure, but overall demonstrates a favorable response to therapy.
Current heart failure therapy includes sacubitril/valsartan, metoprolol succinate, and empagliflozin. The patient reports excellent adherence overall and is tolerating therapy well. He reports occasional mild positional dizziness when standing quickly; however, symptoms are infrequent, self-limited, and have not resulted in falls, syncope, or injury. Current blood pressure and heart rate remain appropriate and do not warrant de-escalation of therapy.
Laboratory evaluation demonstrates stable renal function (SCr 1.1 mg/dL, eGFR 80 mL/min/1.73m²) and normal potassium (4.5 mEq/L), indicating no current contraindication to further optimization of GDMT. The patient is not currently receiving mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist therapy despite meeting eligibility criteria. According to the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Heart Failure Guideline, patients with symptomatic HFrEF should receive all four foundational medication classes when tolerated. Initiation of spironolactone is therefore appropriate at this time. Additional future opportunities include titration of sacubitril/valsartan and metoprolol succinate toward maximally tolerated doses.
The patient has also demonstrated meaningful improvement in self-management behaviors since the previous visit. He is now performing daily weight monitoring, checking blood pressure regularly, increasing physical activity, and actively attempting to reduce dietary sodium intake. Continued reinforcement of these behaviors is warranted given their importance in long-term heart failure management.
Hypertension – Controlled
Blood pressure is currently controlled at 118/72 mmHg and home readings are consistently near goal. Current values support continued optimization of heart failure therapy. No additional antihypertensive intervention is indicated at this time beyond ongoing monitoring during GDMT titration.
Preventive Care Gaps
Potential preventive care gaps remain, including lack of documented pneumococcal and Shingrix vaccination. These issues are not the primary focus of today's visit but should be addressed during future follow-up encounters as part of comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction and preventive care.
