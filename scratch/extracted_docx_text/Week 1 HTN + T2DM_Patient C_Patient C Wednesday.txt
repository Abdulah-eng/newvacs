1. Chronic Kidney Disease Stage 3a with Severely Increased Albuminuria – Improving but Persistent Very High Risk
The patient has CKD Stage G3a with an eGFR of 50 mL/min/1.73m² and persistent severely increased albuminuria with a UACR of 330 mg/g, consistent with A3 albuminuria. Although albuminuria has improved significantly from 452 mg/g to 330 mg/g following optimization of therapy, the patient remains within the A3 category. According to KDIGO and the 2026 ADA Standards of Care, persistent severe albuminuria remains a major predictor of CKD progression, cardiovascular events, and future kidney failure.
The observed reduction in albuminuria suggests that the prior therapeutic strategy was effective and that progression risk has likely been reduced. However, substantial residual risk remains due to persistent albuminuria, diabetes, hypertension, CKD, and established ASCVD. Additional optimization of cardiorenal protective therapy should continue to be considered.
2. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus – Improving but Above Goal
The patient's A1C has improved from 8.2% to 7.9% following improved lifestyle behaviors and continued adherence to metformin, empagliflozin, and semaglutide therapy. According to the 2026 ADA Standards of Care, an A1C target of less than 7% is appropriate for many healthy older adults with preserved functional status and reasonable life expectancy.
Although glycemic control remains above goal, the patient demonstrates meaningful improvement and is moving in the desired direction. Current therapy includes agents with established cardiovascular and renal benefit. Additional optimization may be appropriate; however, treatment decisions should continue to prioritize therapies that provide both glycemic improvement and cardiorenal protection.
3. Hypertension – Controlled
The patient's blood pressure has improved from 142/84 mmHg at the initial visit to 130/78 mmHg with repeat measurement of 128/78 mmHg. Home blood pressure monitoring demonstrates similar control. According to the 2025 ACC/AHA Hypertension Guideline, ADA Standards of Care, and KDIGO recommendations, a blood pressure target of less than 130/80 mmHg is appropriate in patients with diabetes, CKD, and elevated cardiovascular risk.
Improved blood pressure control likely contributed to the reduction in albuminuria and represents an important component of ongoing kidney protection. Current therapy is effective and well tolerated. Continued monitoring and maintenance of blood pressure control remain essential.
4. Secondary ASCVD Prevention – Residual Risk Remains
The patient has established ASCVD with a history of STEMI requiring PCI. Despite improvement in overall risk factor management, she remains at very high cardiovascular risk due to diabetes, CKD, obesity, and prior myocardial infarction.
LDL-C has improved modestly from 92 mg/dL to 86 mg/dL but remains above ideal secondary prevention targets. According to ADA and ACC/AHA recommendations, aggressive lipid-lowering strategies should be considered in patients with established ASCVD and multiple high-risk features. Residual cardiovascular risk remains substantial and may represent the next highest-yield target for intervention.
5. Obesity – Improving
The patient's BMI remains elevated at 34.7 kg/m² but weight has decreased since the previous visit. According to the ADA Standards of Care, obesity should be managed as a chronic disease because of its contribution to diabetes progression, hypertension, CKD progression, and cardiovascular risk.

6. Health Literacy Regarding CKD and Cardiorenal Protection – Improving
