ASSESSMENT
Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF) – Stable Symptoms, Assessment Limited by Medication Nonadherence
Angela Brooks is a 64-year-old female with established HFrEF (LVEF 35%) who presents with persistent mild fatigue and exertional dyspnea. At first glance, the patient may appear to be a candidate for additional heart failure optimization; however, further assessment reveals significant adherence concerns that complicate interpretation of treatment response.
The patient reports missing evening doses of sacubitril/valsartan approximately two to three times weekly and occasionally rationing medications because of financial limitations. Because she is not consistently receiving prescribed therapy, it is difficult to determine whether ongoing symptoms represent inadequate treatment response or inadequate medication exposure.
Current renal function, blood pressure, and potassium would likely support future optimization of therapy if indicated; however, adherence barriers should be addressed before recommending additional medication changes. Priority should be placed on improving medication access, adherence, and understanding of treatment goals.
Persistent Nonvalvular Atrial Fibrillation – Elevated Stroke Risk with Intermittent Anticoagulation Nonadherence
The patient has persistent nonvalvular atrial fibrillation and a CHA₂DS₂-VASc score of at least 3 (heart failure, hypertension, female sex), placing her at elevated risk for thromboembolic events and supporting long-term anticoagulation.
The most concerning finding identified during today's visit is intermittent nonadherence to apixaban therapy. The patient reports missing evening doses approximately two to three times weekly and occasionally stretching medication supplies because of cost concerns. She demonstrates limited understanding of the purpose of anticoagulation and was unaware that apixaban is primarily intended to reduce stroke risk associated with atrial fibrillation.
Given the potentially serious consequences of missed anticoagulant doses, adherence improvement and patient education represent immediate priorities. Cost-related barriers should be addressed before considering any modification to the current anticoagulation strategy.
Cost-Related Medication Nonadherence
The patient reports multiple financial barriers affecting medication adherence, particularly with sacubitril/valsartan and apixaban. She lives on a fixed income and occasionally experiences difficulty obtaining refills. Transportation limitations further contribute to medication access challenges.
These barriers are directly affecting implementation of evidence-based therapy and represent a major medication therapy problem. Exploration of insurance coverage, manufacturer assistance programs, medication affordability resources, mail-order pharmacy options, and other access interventions is warranted.
Health Literacy Deficit
The patient demonstrates limited understanding of heart failure, atrial fibrillation, stroke prevention, and the purpose of several prescribed medications. Knowledge deficits appear to contribute to reduced motivation and inconsistent adherence.
Improved education regarding disease states, treatment goals, and expected benefits of therapy may improve long-term adherence and engagement.

SCORING RULES:
FULL: Input present, accurate, and clinically contextualized (not merely a keyword).

ZERO: Absent, or a factually incorrect statement is made about that input.
Additivity: Points are additive within each subsection → subsections additive within sections → sections additive to 100.0.
Acceptable-alternative inputs (e.g., therapeutic choices) list every guideline-supported answer in parentheses; any listed answer earns full credit.
