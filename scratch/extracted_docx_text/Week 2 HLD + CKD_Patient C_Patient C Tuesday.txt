1. Chronic Kidney Disease (G3bA3) – High Risk for Progression
The patient has CKD G3bA3 based on eGFR 42 mL/min/1.73m² and severely increased albuminuria (UACR 520 mg/g). Persistent severe albuminuria and reduced kidney function place the patient at substantial risk for future kidney disease progression, cardiovascular events, and kidney failure.
According to the KDIGO 2024 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Evaluation and Management of Chronic Kidney Disease, both eGFR and albuminuria should be incorporated into CKD staging because both independently predict kidney and cardiovascular outcomes. The patient's combination of G3b kidney function and A3 albuminuria represents a significantly elevated risk category.
Despite ACE inhibitor and SGLT2 inhibitor therapy, substantial residual kidney disease risk remains.
2. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus with CKD – Reasonably Controlled but Persistent Cardiorenal Risk
The patient's A1C is 7.2%, suggesting reasonable glycemic control. He reports adherence to metformin and empagliflozin and denies hypoglycemia.
According to KDIGO 2024, management of diabetes in CKD extends beyond glycemic control and includes therapies that reduce kidney and cardiovascular risk. Although glycemic control is acceptable, persistent severe albuminuria indicates ongoing cardiorenal risk that is not fully addressed by glucose lowering alone.

4. Hyperlipidemia / Elevated ASCVD Risk
The patient remains at elevated cardiovascular risk due to diabetes, CKD G3bA3, hypertension, obesity, family history of premature ASCVD, and persistent albuminuria.
LDL-C is 88 mg/dL while receiving rosuvastatin 20 mg daily.
According to the 2026 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Management of Blood Cholesterol, CKD and diabetes are important risk-enhancing conditions that substantially increase ASCVD risk.
