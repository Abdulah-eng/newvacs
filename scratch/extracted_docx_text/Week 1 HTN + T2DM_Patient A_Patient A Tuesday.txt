ASSESSMENT
Hypertension – Uncontrolled
The patient's blood pressure remains above the guideline-recommended goal of less than 130/80 mmHg for adults with hypertension and diabetes according to the 2025 AHA/ACC Hypertension Guideline. Office blood pressure today was 146/92 mmHg with repeat measurement of 144/90 mmHg, and home readings appear consistent with office values. Multiple contributors to uncontrolled blood pressure were identified during today's visit, including medication nonadherence, improper home blood pressure monitoring technique, obesity, frequent restaurant meals likely contributing to excess sodium intake, and recurrent NSAID use. The patient reports missing antihypertensive medications approximately two to three times weekly, which likely contributes to persistently elevated readings. Current antihypertensive therapy consists of low-dose lisinopril and hydrochlorothiazide, both of which are tolerated without adverse effects. Renal function and serum potassium remain within acceptable limits for ACE inhibitor dose escalation. Although adherence concerns must be addressed, persistent elevations in both clinic and home readings support pharmacologic intensification in addition to lifestyle modification and adherence interventions.
Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus – Uncontrolled
The patient's diabetes is currently uncontrolled as evidenced by an A1C of 8.2%, representing progressive worsening from 7.3% one year ago and 7.6% six months ago. Current glycemic control exceeds the ADA 2026 recommended target of less than 7% for most nonpregnant adults. Several factors likely contribute to worsening glycemic control, including medication nonadherence, obesity, physical inactivity, dietary habits, and inconsistent glucose monitoring. The patient remains on a submaximal dose of metformin despite preserved renal function and reports good tolerability. Because adherence barriers and lifestyle factors have not yet been fully addressed, optimization of metformin therapy is appropriate before advancing to additional glucose-lowering medications. However, future consideration of a GLP-1 receptor agonist may be appropriate given the patient's obesity, desire for weight loss, and need for additional glycemic control if target A1C is not achieved.
Chronic Kidney Disease Risk / Missing Albuminuria Screening
The patient has both diabetes and hypertension but does not have a documented urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio available for review. Current renal function is preserved with an eGFR of 79 mL/min/1.73m²; however, absence of albuminuria data prevents complete assessment of kidney disease risk and limits optimization of renal-protective therapy. Current guidelines recommend routine albuminuria screening in patients with diabetes. A UACR should be obtained to establish baseline renal risk and guide future treatment decisions.
Hyperlipidemia and Primary ASCVD Prevention
The patient is a 54-year-old female with diabetes, hypertension, obesity, elevated triglycerides, and a family history of cardiovascular disease who is currently not receiving statin therapy. LDL-C remains elevated at 112 mg/dL. According to ADA 2026 recommendations, adults aged 40–75 years with diabetes should receive at least moderate-intensity statin therapy for primary prevention. The patient possesses multiple ASCVD risk enhancers that further support statin initiation. Failure to initiate statin therapy represents a significant evidence-based gap in cardiovascular risk reduction.
Obesity
The patient's BMI of 34.8 kg/m² is consistent with class I obesity and likely contributes to worsening hypertension, insulin resistance, and overall cardiovascular risk. The patient expresses strong motivation to lose weight and identifies this as her primary health goal. This motivation provides an opportunity to improve engagement with lifestyle interventions and may influence future antihyperglycemic medication selection.
Chronic NSAID Use
The patient reports using ibuprofen several times weekly for osteoarthritis-related knee pain. Chronic NSAID use may contribute to elevated blood pressure, reduced antihypertensive efficacy, and increased future renal risk. Alternative pain management strategies should be explored to reduce long-term cardiovascular and renal complications.
Preventive Care Gaps
The patient appears to have several potential immunization gaps, including pneumococcal vaccination, herpes zoster vaccination, and Tdap booster administration. Further review of immunization records is warranted to ensure guideline-directed preventive care is provided.
