Week 5 Overview: Depression + Anxiety + Tobacco Cessation
Why These Disease States Are Paired
Depression, anxiety, and tobacco use disorder are among the most common behavioral health conditions managed in ambulatory care and frequently coexist in the same patient.
Depression and anxiety often occur together and can worsen symptom severity, impair daily functioning, reduce medication adherence, and negatively impact quality of life.
Patients with behavioral health conditions also use tobacco products at significantly higher rates than the general population. Many patients report smoking to manage stress, anxiety, or depressive symptoms, creating a cycle that can worsen both physical and mental health outcomes.
Together these conditions significantly increase the risk of:
• Functional impairment
• Poor medication adherence
• Reduced quality of life
• Substance use disorders
• Cardiovascular disease
• Chronic disease complications
• Healthcare utilization
• Premature mortality
Students must learn to manage all three conditions simultaneously rather than in isolation.
Learning Objectives
By the end of Week 5 students should be able to:
• Apply depression guidelines to patient-specific scenarios.
• Apply anxiety guidelines to patient-specific scenarios.
• Apply tobacco cessation guidelines to patient-specific scenarios.
• Interpret PHQ-9 scores.
• Interpret GAD-7 scores.
• Develop evidence-based treatment plans.
• Select appropriate antidepressant therapy.
• Assess medication adherence and treatment response.
• Identify treatment-resistant depression.
• Assess readiness to quit tobacco use.
• Recommend evidence-based smoking cessation therapies.
• Perform patient-centered counseling.
• Create complete SOAP notes.
• Develop monitoring and follow-up plans.

Longitudinal Patients
Patient A
Foundational case focused on uncomplicated depression, anxiety, and tobacco use disorder management.
Patient B
Adherence and access case focused on medication barriers, missed doses, limited counseling access, and practical problem solving.
Patient C
Higher-risk patient requiring management of persistent symptoms, treatment optimization, treatment-resistant depression concepts, and tobacco dependence.
Progression of Complexity
Patient A
Focus on foundational diagnosis and treatment.
Students will learn:
• Initial antidepressant selection
• PHQ-9 interpretation
• GAD-7 interpretation
• CBT referral
• Tobacco use assessment
Patient B
Focus on adherence and barriers.
Students will learn:
• Medication adherence assessment
• Cost barriers
• Transportation barriers
• Missed appointments
• Behavioral health access challenges
• Tobacco cessation obstacles
Patient C
Focus on advanced clinical decision making.
Students will learn:
• Partial response management
• Treatment optimization
• Escalation strategies
• Treatment-resistant depression concepts
• Persistent tobacco use
• Long-term management planning
The goal is not simply to know the guidelines.
The goal is to apply the guidelines to realistic ambulatory care patient encounters.
Weekly Workflow
Monday: Guideline Foundation Day
Students will review:
• Depression guideline summary
• Anxiety guideline summary
• Tobacco cessation guideline summary
• Required readings and resources
The purpose of Monday is to establish the evidence base that will be applied throughout the week.
Tuesday: Initial Patient Assessment
Students will:
• Conduct patient interviews
• Review chart information
• Interpret PHQ-9 and GAD-7 scores
• Identify medication therapy problems
• Assess tobacco use
• Develop initial treatment plans
• Complete SOAP note documentation
Wednesday: Follow-Up Assessment
Students will:
• Evaluate treatment response
• Reassess PHQ-9 and GAD-7 scores
• Assess adherence
• Assess adverse effects
• Evaluate smoking cessation progress
• Modify treatment plans as needed
• Complete SOAP note documentation
Thursday: Advanced Clinical Decision-Making
Students will:
• Manage complex medication therapy problems
• Address patient-specific barriers
• Optimize antidepressant therapy
• Evaluate persistent symptoms
• Apply guideline recommendations to challenging scenarios
• Complete SOAP note documentation
Friday: Reflection and Journal Club
Students will:
• Reflect on patient care experiences
• Apply literature to clinical practice
• Participate in journal club discussion
• Defend evidence-based treatment recommendations
