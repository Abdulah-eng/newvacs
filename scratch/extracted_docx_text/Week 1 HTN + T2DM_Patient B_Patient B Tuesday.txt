ASSESSMENT
Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus – Uncontrolled Secondary to Medication Access Barriers and Suboptimal Adherence
The patient's diabetes is currently uncontrolled as evidenced by an A1C of 8.8%, which has progressively increased from 8.0% one year ago and 8.4% six months ago. Current glycemic control exceeds the ADA 2026 recommended target of less than 7% for most nonpregnant adults. While medication intensification may initially appear appropriate, the most clinically significant finding from today's visit is that the patient never initiated empagliflozin therapy due to affordability concerns. Therefore, the patient has not received the full treatment plan previously intended by his primary care provider.
Additional contributors to poor glycemic control include medication stretching, delayed refills, obesity, physical inactivity, frequent consumption of convenience foods, sugary beverages, and limited understanding of diabetes management. Renal function remains preserved and there are no contraindications to SGLT2 inhibitor therapy. Before concluding that additional pharmacologic escalation is necessary, efforts should focus on improving medication access, affordability, adherence, and understanding of current therapies.
Hypertension – Uncontrolled
The patient's blood pressure remains above the guideline-recommended goal of less than 130/80 mmHg for adults with diabetes according to the 2025 AHA/ACC Hypertension Guideline. Office blood pressure today was 152/94 mmHg with repeat measurement of 150/92 mmHg, and home readings demonstrate similar elevations. Current antihypertensive therapy includes lisinopril 20 mg daily and hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily.
Although medication intensification may ultimately be warranted, assessment today revealed medication stretching behavior and delayed refills due to cost concerns. Consequently, true therapeutic effectiveness of the current regimen remains difficult to assess. Improving medication access and adherence should be prioritized before major antihypertensive regimen modifications are made. Lifestyle factors including obesity, poor dietary habits, and physical inactivity likely contribute to persistent blood pressure elevation.
Hyperlipidemia and Primary ASCVD Prevention – Suboptimally Controlled
The patient has diabetes, hypertension, obesity, former tobacco use, family history of stroke, and is currently receiving atorvastatin 20 mg daily. LDL-C remains above optimal levels at 103 mg/dL. However, medication adherence concerns identified during today's visit make it difficult to determine whether current therapy has been consistently utilized. Improving adherence should be prioritized before additional lipid-lowering intensification is considered. The patient's overall cardiovascular risk remains elevated and long-term preventive strategies remain important.
Medication Access and Affordability Barrier
The most significant medication-related problem identified during today's encounter is inability to access prescribed therapy due to cost. The patient reports that empagliflozin was never started because the quoted monthly cost exceeded $500. Furthermore, the patient has delayed refills and stretched medications due to affordability concerns. These findings demonstrate that medication access, rather than therapeutic failure alone, is a major contributor to uncontrolled chronic disease.
Failure to identify and address these barriers would likely result in inappropriate medication intensification and continued poor disease control. Medication affordability should be considered a primary treatment target during today's encounter.
Medication Adherence – Suboptimal
The patient initially reported taking medications regularly; however, further questioning revealed delayed refills, medication stretching, and non-initiation of prescribed therapy. These findings suggest adherence is substantially lower than initially reported. Financial concerns appear to be the primary driver of adherence difficulties. Continued assessment and implementation of adherence-support strategies are warranted.
Obesity
The patient's BMI of 31.9 kg/m² is consistent with obesity and contributes to hypertension, insulin resistance, and elevated cardiovascular risk. Physical activity is limited and dietary habits remain suboptimal. Weight reduction may improve both blood pressure and glycemic control.
Health Literacy Deficit
The patient demonstrates limited understanding of A1C, medication purpose, and the role of empagliflozin in diabetes management. He incorrectly assumed that the medication was optional because of its cost and did not understand the importance of notifying healthcare providers when affordability barriers arose. Ongoing education is needed to improve self-management and engagement in care.
Preventive Care Gaps
The patient appears overdue for pneumococcal vaccination, recombinant zoster vaccination, and Tdap booster administration. Preventive care should be incorporated into long-term management.
