Journal Club Key
AZALEA-TIMI 71 Trial: Abelacimab versus Rivaroxaban in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation
Piccini JP, Patel MR, Mahaffey KW, et al. Abelacimab versus Rivaroxaban in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation. N Engl J Med. 2024;391:105-116.
Background
• Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia encountered in ambulatory care practice.
• AF significantly increases the risk of ischemic stroke, systemic embolism, heart failure progression, hospitalization, and mortality.
• Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) have largely replaced warfarin because of improved safety, fewer drug interactions, and reduced monitoring requirements.
• Despite these advantages, bleeding remains the major limitation of current anticoagulation therapy.
• Many patients discontinue anticoagulation because of bleeding concerns, resulting in increased stroke risk.
• Factor XI has emerged as a promising therapeutic target because it appears to contribute substantially to thrombosis while playing a smaller role in normal hemostasis.
• Abelacimab is a monoclonal antibody that inhibits Factor XI and Factor XIa activity.
• The AZALEA-TIMI 71 Trial was designed to evaluate whether Factor XI inhibition could reduce bleeding compared with standard DOAC therapy in patients with atrial fibrillation.
Study Objective
To compare bleeding outcomes between abelacimab and rivaroxaban in patients with atrial fibrillation who require long-term anticoagulation for stroke prevention.
Methods
Study Design
• International
• Multicenter
• Randomized
• Parallel-group trial
Funding Source
• Anthos Therapeutics
Patient Population
• Adults with atrial fibrillation
• Elevated thromboembolic risk requiring chronic anticoagulation
Major Inclusion Criteria
• Age ≥55 years
• Documented atrial fibrillation
• CHA₂DS₂-VASc score requiring anticoagulation
• Appropriate candidate for DOAC therapy
Major Exclusion Criteria
• Mechanical heart valve
• Moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis
• Active bleeding
• Significant contraindication to anticoagulation
• Severe renal impairment
Drug Treatment
Treatment Group 1
• Abelacimab 150 mg subcutaneous monthly
Treatment Group 2
• Abelacimab 90 mg subcutaneous monthly
Control Group
• Rivaroxaban 20 mg daily
• Dose-adjusted when appropriate
Primary Endpoint
Composite of:
• Major bleeding
OR
• Clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding
Secondary Endpoints
• Ischemic stroke
• Systemic embolism
• Death
• Composite cardiovascular outcomes
Safety Outcomes
• Major bleeding
• Clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding
• Serious adverse events
• Injection-site reactions
Statistics
• Intention-to-treat analysis
• Event-driven trial
• Independent event adjudication
• Prespecified interim monitoring
Data Collection / Frequency / Assessment Tools
• Scheduled clinic visits
• Adverse event monitoring
• Bleeding assessments
• Stroke and systemic embolism monitoring
• Independent endpoint adjudication
Results
Follow-Up
• Trial stopped early following recommendation from the independent data monitoring committee because of overwhelming reductions in bleeding events.
Baseline Subject Characteristics
• Mean age approximately 74 years
• High-risk atrial fibrillation population
• Elevated CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores
• Baseline characteristics balanced between groups
Primary Endpoint
Major bleeding or clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding occurred significantly less frequently with abelacimab than with rivaroxaban.
Compared with rivaroxaban:
• Abelacimab 150 mg reduced bleeding by approximately 67%
• Abelacimab 90 mg reduced bleeding by approximately 62%
Secondary Outcomes
Stroke and Systemic Embolism
• Event rates were low across all groups.
• Trial was not powered to establish superiority or noninferiority for stroke prevention outcomes.
Mortality
• No major differences observed.
Safety Outcomes
Major Bleeding
• Significantly lower with abelacimab.
Clinically Relevant Nonmajor Bleeding
• Significantly lower with abelacimab.
Serious Adverse Events
• Similar between groups.
Overall Safety
• Favorable safety profile observed.
Author's Conclusion
Among patients with atrial fibrillation, abelacimab resulted in substantially less bleeding than rivaroxaban.
Strengths
• Novel anticoagulation mechanism
• Randomized controlled design
• Clinically meaningful bleeding outcomes
• Contemporary DOAC comparator
• High-risk AF population
• Strong ambulatory care relevance
• Potential paradigm-shifting anticoagulation strategy
Limitations
• Trial stopped early
• Not powered to establish superiority for stroke prevention
• Long-term efficacy remains uncertain
• Novel therapy not currently available for routine clinical use
• Industry funded
• Limited long-term outcome data
Presenter's Conclusion / Clinical Application
AZALEA-TIMI 71 represents one of the most important anticoagulation trials published in recent years.
The study suggests that Factor XI inhibition may substantially reduce bleeding compared with currently available DOAC therapy while maintaining promising antithrombotic activity.
For pharmacists, this trial reinforces the importance of evaluating:
• Stroke risk
• Bleeding risk
• Patient preferences
• Medication adherence
• Long-term safety
when making anticoagulation recommendations.
Although abelacimab is not currently guideline-directed therapy, AZALEA-TIMI 71 provides insight into the future direction of anticoagulation management and highlights ongoing efforts to separate thrombosis prevention from bleeding risk.
Patients who may potentially benefit from future Factor XI inhibitor therapies include those with:
• Atrial fibrillation
• Elevated stroke risk
• Prior bleeding events
• High bleeding risk
• Difficulty maintaining long-term anticoagulation because of bleeding concerns

Five Clinical Application Questions
A 78-year-old patient with atrial fibrillation has experienced multiple episodes of epistaxis while taking apixaban and is considering stopping anticoagulation altogether. How does AZALEA-TIMI 71 influence future anticoagulation discussions?
Answer:
The trial suggests that future Factor XI inhibitors may provide stroke prevention with substantially less bleeding than current anticoagulants. Although these therapies are not yet standard of care, the study highlights a potential future option for patients whose primary concern is bleeding risk.
Why was rivaroxaban a more clinically relevant comparator than warfarin?
Answer:
DOACs are current first-line therapy for most patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation. Comparing abelacimab against rivaroxaban provides information that is more applicable to contemporary practice than comparison with warfarin.
A patient asks whether abelacimab should replace apixaban today. How would you respond?
Answer:
No. Although AZALEA-TIMI 71 demonstrated impressive reductions in bleeding, additional efficacy data, regulatory approval, and guideline incorporation are still needed before routine clinical use.
Which patient would potentially derive greater benefit from future Factor XI inhibitor therapy?
Patient A
• CHA₂DS₂-VASc = 3
• No history of bleeding
Patient B
• CHA₂DS₂-VASc = 4
• Prior gastrointestinal bleed
• Recurrent epistaxis
Answer:
Patient B. The major potential advantage of Factor XI inhibition is maintaining thromboembolic protection while reducing bleeding risk.
How does AZALEA-TIMI 71 support shared decision-making in anticoagulation management?
Answer:
The trial highlights that patients value both stroke prevention and bleeding avoidance. Future anticoagulation strategies may allow more individualized discussions that better balance these competing risks.
